# Final Review — Delivered Partnership Proposal

**Verdict:** Pass

## Checklist (Corrections Applied)
All 12 requested corrections appear correctly applied:
1) React Native specified. 2) 12-week MVP timeline. 3) IP ownership: Delivered owns app; Randy owns equity. 4) AC relationship stated as confirmed. 5) License expiration (3/5/2026) is prominent. 6) No Apple Pay references. 7) Three revenue scenarios included. 8) MA advertising compliance (935 CMR 500.105) included. 9) Opens with hacked website. 10) “Why Partnership, Not a Dev Shop” present. 11) Seed round (not Series A). 12) Acknowledges Delivered’s existing business.

## Founder Fit (Tone & Credibility)
The tone is largely respectful and non‑patronizing. The “You don’t need saving. You need a partner.” line lands well. The strongest credibility marker is the explicit acknowledgement of their 2.5 years of operations and warehouse model — that will resonate with a founder.

## Equity Ask vs. Offer
Option A (8–12% + rev share) is heavy but justified **if** Randy is actually delivering the app, growth engine, and supply chain integration. The vesting with cliff tied to app approval is a reasonable protection for the founder. Consider clarifying whether the equity is in parent or a dedicated entity for the app/brand (section 8). Some founders will ask about dilution and whether this equity is founder-common or advisor-class.

## Revenue Projections
Projections read more honest than earlier drafts. The conservative/base/optimistic framing helps. However, Month 12 “Optimistic” $200K and 5,000+ downloads may still feel aggressive for Central MA without more pricing/order volume assumptions. A single line clarifying *orders/day x AOV* for each scenario would strengthen credibility (Section 7).

## What’s Missing / Founder Questions
- **Ops impact:** Who handles expanded staffing/driver ops and inventory working capital as growth accelerates? (Sections 7–10)
- **Integration detail:** How Dutchie/POS integration works and who bears integration costs/time risk (Section 3/10).
- **Governance:** How decisions are made if Randy has equity + rev share (Section 8). A simple governance statement would help.

## Strongest Element
The “Why Partnership, Not a Dev Shop” section is the strongest — clearly articulates the differentiated value beyond app development and aligns incentives.

## Weakest Element
Revenue scenario credibility (Section 7). Without unit economics, even “conservative” feels like optimism. One paragraph on AOV, order frequency, and current baseline would fix this.
