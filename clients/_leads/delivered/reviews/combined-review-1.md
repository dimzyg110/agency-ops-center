## Consistency Check: Proposal vs Brand Vision

**Largely aligned.** No pricing, timeline, or structural contradictions. Both documents cite the same equity terms, 12-week MVP, AC relationship, and 2029 exclusivity window. Two minor discrepancies:

1. **Brand voice mismatch.** The proposal uses casual copy ("Fire's rolling your way 🔥," "Arriving by 4:20") while the brand vision explicitly says **no stoner slang** and targets a "craft brewery / specialty coffee" tone. The brand vision is right — fix the proposal's voice table.
2. **Referral incentive.** Proposal says "give $10, get $10." Brand vision's zone launch playbook says "give $20, get $20." Pick one. $10 is safer for a bootstrapped company; $20 is more competitive. Decide before presenting.

Everything else — revenue projections, zone expansion budget ($11,500), social targets, AC product concepts, compliance framework — is consistent across documents.

---

## Top 3 Strengths (Across Both Docs)

1. **The market thesis is airtight.** Zero delivery apps in MA + 2029 exclusivity lock + 27-operator ceiling = a genuinely rare first-mover window. Both documents articulate this clearly. This is the strongest selling point and neither doc oversells it.

2. **AC vertical integration is the real differentiator.** The brand vision's "From Lowell to Your Door" narrative turns a supply chain deal into emotional brand storytelling. House-brand products at 35%+ margins vs. 15% courier margins is the single most important business lever in this partnership. Both docs nail this.

3. **Partnership structure protects the founders.** $5K upfront, no retainer for 6 months, Delivered owns all IP, clean dissolution terms. For bootstrapped founders, this removes the "we can't afford a tech partner" objection. Smart design.

---

## Top 3 Weaknesses / Risks

1. **The brand vision is too much for the audience.** Plan-3-full is 63K bytes of agency-grade creative strategy (sound design cues, haptic feedback specs, podcast ad scripts). Ruben, Eddy, and Tut are bootstrapped delivery operators. Showing them a 1,900-line brand playbook risks two reactions: (a) overwhelm, or (b) "these guys are going to spend all our money on branding." The content is good — but the *volume* signals misaligned priorities for a pre-revenue partnership.

2. **Social growth targets require budget the proposal doesn't commit to.** The brand vision calls for $500–$2,000/month in paid promotion, 1–2 TikTok videos/day, 5–6 Instagram posts/week, influencer collabs, a podcast, and a 6-episode video series. Who pays for this? The proposal's Option A has $0 retainer for 6 months. Either Roviq absorbs this cost (unsustainable) or it's charged separately (undisclosed). This gap needs to be addressed before the meeting.

3. **The "Midnight Cannabis Lounge" aesthetic (deep charcoal, matte gold) from the proposal conflicts with the brand vision's actual palette (forest green, warm white, sunset amber).** The proposal's Section 4 describes "Brooklyn cocktail bar" while the brand vision describes "local coffee roaster / craft brewery." The brand vision's direction is more honest for a delivery company in Clinton, MA. Kill the "Midnight Cannabis Lounge" framing in the proposal — it oversells the vibe and contradicts the supplement.

## Recommendation: How to Present

**Lead with the proposal. Hold the brand vision in reserve.**

1. **Present:** PROPOSAL-DRAFT.md only, with the voice table and "Midnight Lounge" language cleaned up. This is the business case — market window, app specs, AC deal, partnership terms. It's tight, it respects their time, and it answers the question "why should we do this?"

2. **Reference, don't deliver:** Mention that a full creative strategy exists. Say "we've already developed the brand playbook — color palette, content calendar, zone launch template, multilingual strategy — and we'll walk through it once we agree on the partnership." This signals depth without overwhelming.

3. **Pull selectively if asked:** If founders ask "what would the brand look like?" — show Section 1 (positioning + color palette) and the zone expansion budget table from Section 4. These are concrete and visual. Skip the sound design, haptic feedback, and podcast production details.

4. **Never send plan-3-full unsolicited.** A 63K document from a partner you haven't signed with reads as either (a) they built this before we agreed, which is presumptuous, or (b) they're going to spend months on brand decks instead of building the app.

**Fix before presenting:** Brand voice table in proposal, referral amount, kill "Midnight Cannabis Lounge" phrasing, add one line about who funds content/social execution in the first 6 months.
