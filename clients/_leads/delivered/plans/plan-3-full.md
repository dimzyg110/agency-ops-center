# SECTION 1: BRAND POSITIONING — "DELIVERED" AS THE NAME IN MA CANNABIS DELIVERY

## The Naming Advantage

"Delivered" is, counterintuitively, perfect. It's generic enough to own the entire category but specific enough to trademark and defend. Consider:
- **Band-Aid** = adhesive bandages
- **Kleenex** = facial tissues  
- **Uber** = rideshare
- **Delivered** = cannabis delivery (the goal)

The name doesn't describe *what* you do—it describes the *outcome*. Customers don't want "cannabis delivery services." They want their product **delivered**. This is category-defining positioning.

## Strategic Positioning Statement

> **For cannabis consumers in Massachusetts who value convenience, quality, and discretion, Delivered is the delivery service that brings the best local cannabis directly to your door. Unlike other operators who treat delivery as an afterthought, Delivered was built from day one around the delivery experience—with the state's first native mobile app, exclusive partnerships with Massachusetts cultivators, and a founding team that represents the communities we serve.**

## Brand Voice & Personality

Delivered speaks with a voice that is:

| Trait | Expression | Example |
|-------|------------|---------|
| **Confident but warm** | We know what we're doing, but we're not arrogant | "We've got you. Your order is confirmed and on its way." |
| **Adult but playful** | Mature enough for legal cannabis, light enough to enjoy | "Your Friday night upgrade is arriving in 12 minutes." |
| **Local but elevated** | Proudly Massachusetts, not provincial | "Grown in Lowell. Delivered to Worcester. Enjoyed by you." |
| **Professional but human** | Reliable service, real people behind it | "Your driver Marcus is 3 minutes away with your order." |

**Voice Don'ts:**
- No stoner slang (no "dank," "fire," "loud")
- No medical/clinical language (no "medicate," "treatment")
- No corporate sterility (no "value-add," "synergy")
- No pretending to be something we're not

# SECTION 2: THE APP EXPERIENCE — CREATIVE VISION

## The Feeling We're Creating

Using the Delivered app should feel like:
- Ordering from your favorite local coffee shop (familiar, warm)
- Tracking an Uber (transparent, controlled)
- Unboxing an Apple product (premium, considered)
- Texting a friend who's hooking you up (personal, trusted)

**Not:** Filling out a medical form (Dutchie), browsing a confusing menu (most dispensary sites), or using a clunky delivery app from 2015.

## Onboarding Flow — First-Time User Experience

### The 60-Second Promise

New users should be able to place their first order within 60 seconds of downloading. This is the benchmark.

### Onboarding Sequence

**Step 1: Welcome Screen**
- Full-screen visual of Massachusetts landscape (Berkshires, Cape, Boston skyline—geo-detected)
- Headline: "Massachusetts, Delivered."
- Subhead: "The easiest way to get cannabis delivered."
- CTA: "Get Started" (no signup required yet)

**Step 2: Age Verification (Required by Law)**
- Clear, respectful age gate: "You must be 21+ to use Delivered"
- Simple date entry or "I confirm I am 21+"
- **Compliance Note:** This age gate is required before any cannabis content is shown

**Step 3: Location Permission**
- Contextual request: "We need your location to show available products and delivery times"
- Map preview showing coverage area
- Manual zip code entry as fallback

**Step 4: Quick Preferences (Optional but Encouraged)**
- "What are you into?" (checkboxes: Flower, Edibles, Vapes, Pre-rolls, etc.)
- Used for personalization, not gating
- Can skip: "I'll browse everything"

**Step 5: Browse & Order**
- Direct to product catalog
- No forced account creation before browsing
- Account creation triggered at checkout (reduce friction)

### The "Aha" Moment

The moment of delight happens when:
1. User adds first item to cart → satisfying haptic feedback + subtle animation
2. User sees real-time delivery estimate → "Delivered in 42 minutes"
3. User checks out → simple, clean confirmation screen
4. First push notification arrives → "Your order is on its way!"

The design should feel premium without being pretentious, practical without being boring.

**Key Principles:**

**Cart:**

- Promo code field (prominent—users expect this)

**Tracking (Post-Purchase):**

## Sound Design Cues (Optional, Distinctive)

**Philosophy:** Sound should enhance, not annoy. All sounds optional, toggleable.

| Trigger | Sound Direction | Notes |
|---------|-----------------|-------|
| Order Confirmed | Subtle "swoosh" + gentle chime | Success without being loud |
| Driver Arriving | Soft notification tone | Attention-grabbing but pleasant |
| Order Complete | Satisfying "ding" | Closure sound |
| Error | Gentle "bloop" descending | Attention without panic |

**Technical Note:** React Native supports sound through libraries like `react-native-sound` or Expo's audio API. Keep sounds short (<1 second) and subtle.

## Differentiation from Dutchie

| Aspect | Dutchie Experience | Delivered App Experience |
|--------|-------------------|--------------------------|
| **Interface** | Web-based, form-like | Native app, fluid, designed for mobile |
| **Ordering** | Multi-step, clunky | Streamlined, sub-60-second possible |

## React Native Design Constraints & Opportunities

**Constraints to Design Around:**
- Performance: Complex animations can lag on older devices
- Platform parity: iOS and Android should feel native to each while maintaining brand consistency
- Bundle size: Heavy assets impact download size

**Opportunities to Leverage:**
- Native navigation patterns (bottom tabs on iOS, drawer on Android if desired)
- Platform-specific features (iOS widgets, Android intents)
- Code reuse: One design system, two platforms
- Hot updates: Iterate quickly based on user feedback

**Benchmarks:**
- Average retail: 30-40
