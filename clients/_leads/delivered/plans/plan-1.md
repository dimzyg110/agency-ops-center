## 1. Executive Summary

The Delivered Inc sits at the intersection of three rare advantages: a social equity delivery license in a market protected from new entrants until April 2029, an operational footprint in Central Massachusetts with legal authority to expand statewide, and the distinction of operating in an industry where not a single competitor — including the $2.6M-funded Zyp Run — has launched a native mobile app. Roviq proposes a strategic partnership — not a vendor engagement — to build the first cannabis delivery app in Massachusetts, deploy a programmatic growth engine across every deliverable municipality in the state, and vertically integrate Delivered with Roviq's existing cultivator and brand relationships (Advanced Cultivators and House of Pride) to create a supply-to-doorstep pipeline that no other delivery operator can replicate. This partnership is structured around shared ownership: a small upfront commitment, equity in the combined venture, and revenue share that aligns incentives so both parties win only when the business grows. The window for first-mover advantage is open now — but it won't stay open.

## 2. Market Opportunity

### 2.1 Why Cannabis Delivery in MA Is About to Explode

Massachusetts generated **$1.6 billion** in legal cannabis sales in 2025. Delivery captured just **$20.4 million** of that — **1.27%** of the total market. For context, delivery penetration in mature markets like California exceeds 10-15%. Even a modest shift to 3-4% penetration in MA represents a **$48-64M market** — a 2.5-3x increase from current levels.

The infrastructure is now in place. Consumers have been trained by DoorDash, Instacart, and Amazon to expect delivery. Cannabis is the last major retail category where "drive to a store and wait in line" remains the default. The friction gap between consumer expectation and current cannabis retail experience is enormous.

Three regulatory tailwinds are accelerating this:

1. **CCC is actively researching** allowing delivery into municipalities that currently ban all cannabis retail. There are **180+ towns** in MA that have banned dispensaries — if delivery is permitted into those towns, the addressable market roughly doubles overnight.
2. **Home delivery orders carry higher average basket sizes** than in-store purchases nationally ($75-95 vs. $55-65), driven by minimum order thresholds and the convenience premium.
3. **The warehouse model wins.** CCC data shows delivery operators capture **79% of delivery revenue** compared to courier-model operators. Delivered runs the warehouse model.

### 2.2 The App Gap

This is the single most important competitive insight in the Massachusetts cannabis delivery market:

**Nobody has a mobile app.**

| Operator | Funding | Platform | Mobile App |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zyp Run | $2.6M raised | Proprietary web | ❌ No |
| Rolling Releaf | Unknown | Dutchie web | ❌ No |
| The Delivered Inc | Bootstrapped | Dutchie web | ❌ No |
| Lantern (Curaleaf) | Corporate-backed | Dutchie web | ❌ No |
| All other operators | Various | Dutchie/Jane web | ❌ No |

This is not a gap — it's a canyon. In every other delivery vertical (food, grocery, pharmacy), the mobile app is the primary ordering channel. Cannabis delivery in Massachusetts is stuck in a browser-only era. The first operator to launch a native app with push notifications, one-tap reordering, real-time tracking, and a loyalty program will structurally change their customer retention economics.

A push notification costs **$0.00** to send. An SMS costs **$0.01-0.05**. An Instagram post reaches **5-10%** of followers organically. The app owner has a direct, free, owned communication channel to every customer who downloads it.

### 2.3 The Statewide Expansion Opportunity

Delivered currently operates in ~30 towns in Central MA (Worcester, Leominster, Fitchburg, etc.). But the license permits delivery to **any municipality that allows adult-use retail sales**. That's approximately **200+ towns** across the state.

The constraint isn't legal — it's operational and logistical. You need:
- Product inventory (solved by the warehouse model + wholesale relationships)
- Delivery drivers in the target zone (solved by hiring, which follows revenue)
- Customer awareness in new markets (solved by programmatic SEO + app presence)

The expansion playbook is straightforward: launch digitally in a new zone (SEO pages, app availability, targeted ads), test demand with existing driver capacity from the nearest base, and spin up dedicated drivers when order volume justifies it. This is the same playbook every food delivery company used — digital presence first, operations follow demand.

### 2.4 The Social Equity Moat

On [date of vote], the Cannabis Control Commission voted **4-0** to maintain delivery license exclusivity for social equity and economic empowerment applicants through **April 2029**. This means:

- No new non-equity delivery licenses will be issued for 3+ more years
- Large MSOs (Curaleaf, Trulieve, Green Thumb) cannot launch their own delivery operations
- The 27 active delivery operators have a **protected market** with a known, fixed number of competitors
- Any investment in Delivered's growth compounds in a market where competitive entry is structurally blocked

This is the regulatory equivalent of a patent. The 27 operators who are active today are the only ones who will be active until at least 2029. Market share gained now is defensible for years.

### 2.5 Why NOW Is the Moment

Five factors converge right now:

1. **The app gap is temporary.** Zyp Run has $2.6M in funding. It's only a matter of time before they or another well-funded operator builds an app. First-mover advantage has a shelf life.
2. **Delivered's Central MA position is strong but capped.** Without statewide expansion and new customer acquisition channels, growth will plateau against the ~30-town ceiling.
3. **The CCC delivery-to-banned-towns decision** could come within 12 months. Being positioned with statewide infrastructure when that door opens is worth more than scrambling to build it after.
4. **AC and HOP are active NOW.** The supply chain integration opportunity exists today. Exclusive products, house brands, and vertical margin capture are available immediately.
5. **The site hack is actively damaging SEO.** Every day the casino spam pages remain in Google's index, Delivered loses organic authority. This is an urgent fix that also creates a natural entry point for the broader partnership.

## 4. The Mobile App Vision

### 4.1 First Cannabis Delivery App in Massachusetts

The app isn't just a feature — it's the centerpiece of the entire growth strategy and the asset that makes this partnership fundable.

**Why native iOS (Swift), not a web wrapper:**
- Push notifications work reliably (web push has ~60% opt-in vs. ~70-80% for native)
- Home screen presence = top-of-mind brand positioning
- Faster performance, smoother animations, better UX
- App Store presence provides organic discovery
- Apple Pay integration for frictionless checkout
- Location services for accurate delivery estimates

**Android timeline:** iOS first (higher cannabis spend per user nationally), Android within 3-6 months of iOS validation via React Native or native Kotlin build.

### 4.2 Core Features

**Ordering**
- Full product catalog with high-res photos, lab results, terpene profiles
- Smart search with filters (strain type, THC%, price, brand, effects)
- Saved favorites and recently ordered
- Cart with real-time inventory verification

**Real-Time Tracking**
- Live driver location on map (think Uber Eats)
- Estimated delivery time with dynamic updates
- Driver contact (in-app messaging, not exposed phone numbers)
- Delivery confirmation with photo

**Push Notifications**
- Order status updates
- Flash sales and limited drops
- Restock alerts for favorited products
- Personalized recommendations based on purchase history
- Loyalty milestone notifications

**Loyalty Program**
- Points per dollar spent
- Tier system (Bronze → Silver → Gold → Platinum)
- Tier-specific perks (free delivery, early access to drops, exclusive products)
- Referral bonuses (give $10, get $10)
- Birthday rewards

**One-Tap Reorder**
- "Order Again" for previous orders
- Subscription option for regular purchases (weekly, biweekly, monthly)
- Smart reorder reminders based on estimated consumption patterns

### 4.3 AC Exclusive Products In-App

This is the vertical integration play that makes the app genuinely different:

- **"Delivered Originals"** — house-brand prerolls and flower manufactured by AC, available ONLY through the Delivered app
- **HOP Collection** — curated House of Pride products as app-exclusive offerings
- **Limited Drops** — small-batch, limited-quantity releases that are only available to app users, creating urgency and exclusivity
- **Price advantage** — because Delivered captures wholesale margin on house-brand products, they can price them 10-20% below comparable third-party products while maintaining higher margins

This creates a flywheel: exclusive products drive app downloads → app users have higher retention and order frequency → higher volume justifies more exclusive product development → more exclusive products drive more downloads.

### 4.4 Statewide Scalability

The app is built once and scales everywhere. When Delivered expands to a new delivery zone:
- The app automatically shows available products for that zone
- Push notifications can target users by location
- Zone-specific promotions can launch instantly
- No new infrastructure needed — just toggle the zone on

One app. One codebase. Every market in Massachusetts.

### 4.5 The Data Asset

Every app interaction generates data that has compounding value:

- **Customer preferences**: What strains, potencies, and product types each user prefers
- **Purchase patterns**: Order frequency, average basket size, price sensitivity
- **Reorder behavior**: Which products have repeat purchase rates, what the typical reorder interval is
- **Geographic demand**: Where orders cluster, where unserved demand exists, where to expand next
- **Promotion response**: Which discounts drive incremental orders vs. just discounting orders that would have happened anyway

This data becomes the foundation for:
- Intelligent inventory management (stock what sells, cut what doesn't)
- Dynamic pricing optimization
- Targeted marketing that actually works
- **VC pitch materials** — "We have 15,000 app users, $95 average order value, 2.3 orders per month per active user, and 40% month-over-month retention"

### 4.6 The VC Story This Enables

Without the app, Delivered is a delivery company with a WordPress site. Solid but unexceptional.

With the app, the pitch becomes:

> "We're the only cannabis delivery operator in Massachusetts with a native mobile app, exclusive house-brand products, and a vertically integrated supply chain — operating in a market protected from new entrants until 2029. We have [X] app downloads, [Y] monthly active users, and [$Z] in app-driven monthly revenue growing at [Q]% month-over-month."

That's a fundable story. That's a story that justifies a $2-5M seed round — the same range Zyp Run raised, but with a stronger moat (app + exclusive products + regulatory protection + vertical integration vs. Zyp Run's web-only platform with no product differentiation).

## 6. Deal Structure Options

### Guiding Principles

Before the specific options, these principles apply to all structures:

1. **Alignment of incentives.** Both parties should make more money when the business grows. Neither party should have a windfall if the other gets nothing.
2. **Protection for both sides.** Randy shouldn't work for free if the partnership dissolves. Delivered shouldn't give away equity for work that doesn't materialize.
3. **Milestone-based progression.** Equity and revenue share should vest or activate based on actual deliverables and results, not just time.
4. **Clean for future investment.** The deal structure shouldn't scare off future investors or create cap table problems.

---

### Option A: "Startup Partner"

**Best for:** Maximum alignment, highest upside for both sides, appropriate when Delivered views Roviq as a true co-builder.

| Component | Terms |
|---|---|
| **Upfront Fee** | $5,000 (covers Month 1 triage: hack cleanup, security, SEO audit) |
| **Equity** | 8-12% of The Delivered Inc (or a jointly formed app entity) |
| **Vesting** | 4-year vest with 1-year cliff, acceleration on change of control |
| **Revenue Share** | 5% of gross revenue above $75,000/month (kicks in only after Delivered is healthy) |
| **Monthly Retainer** | $0 for first 6 months; $2,500/month starting Month 7 (offset against revenue share) |
| **Randy's Role** | CTO + Growth Partner — owns technology and marketing strategy |

**The logic:** The $5,000 upfront covers Randy's real costs for Month 1 (server time, tools, security services) and signals mutual seriousness without being a barrier. The equity is the primary compensation for the app build (market value: $80-150K) and ongoing growth engine. The revenue share only kicks in above $75K/month — meaning Delivered is already doing well before Randy takes a percentage. The 6-month retainer holiday gives the partnership time to produce results before any recurring cash obligation.

**Revenue share trigger math:** $75K/month is approximately where Delivered would be if they grow 30-40% from estimated current revenue. Below that threshold, Roviq earns nothing beyond the upfront $5K — all upside is in equity appreciation. Above it, 5% on incremental revenue = $1,250/month at $100K revenue, $3,750/month at $150K, $6,250/month at $200K. This scales reasonably and never becomes punitive.

**Protections for Delivered:**
- Equity vests over 4 years — if Randy disappears after 6 months, he retains only ~1.5-3% (post-cliff vested amount)
- Revenue share has a floor — no payment until Delivered is succeeding
- Defined deliverables for each phase — missed milestones can pause vesting
- Standard "good leaver / bad leaver" provisions

**Protections for Randy:**
- Equity is issued at signing (vesting, not options — actual ownership with vesting restrictions)
- $5K upfront means he's not underwater from day one
- Change of control acceleration: if Delivered is acquired, unvested equity accelerates to 50-100% vested
- IP assignment only upon full vest — if partnership dissolves before cliff, app IP reverts to Roviq
- Revenue share survives partnership dissolution for 24 months (if the growth engine Randy built continues generating revenue, he continues earning)

**When this makes sense:** When Delivered's founders see Roviq/Randy as a co-founder-level contributor and are willing to share meaningful equity in exchange for ~$100K+ in contributed value (app + growth engine + supply chain).

### Option B: "Growth Engine"

**Best for:** If Delivered wants Roviq's capabilities but prefers to keep equity tighter. More cash, less equity.

| Component | Terms |
|---|---|
| **Upfront Fee** | $15,000 (covers hack cleanup + app development deposit) |
| **Equity** | 4-6% of The Delivered Inc |
| **Vesting** | 3-year vest with 6-month cliff |
| **Revenue Share** | 8% of app-driven revenue only (orders placed through the app, tracked via attribution) |
| **Monthly Retainer** | $3,000/month starting Month 2 (covers ongoing SEO, content, campaigns) |
| **Roviq Role** | Fractional CMO + Tech Partner |

**The logic:** Higher upfront and monthly payments reduce Roviq's risk and compensate for lower equity. The revenue share is higher (8% vs. 5%) but narrower — only app-driven revenue, not total revenue. This creates a clean incentive: Roviq is laser-focused on making the app successful because that's where their revenue share comes from.

**Revenue share trigger math:** If the app drives $30K/month in orders by Month 6, Roviq earns $2,400/month in rev share + $3,000 retainer = $5,400/month. If app-driven revenue reaches $80K/month by Month 12, rev share = $6,400/month. This is attractive but bounded — Roviq only earns on what the app specifically generates.

**Attribution model:** Orders placed through the iOS app are "app-driven revenue." Orders placed on the website are not. This is clean and auditable — the app has its own order flow, separate from Dutchie web orders.

**Protections for Delivered:**
- Lower equity exposure (4-6% vs. 8-12%)
- Revenue share only on app revenue — if the app fails, Roviq earns rev share on $0
- Monthly retainer can be paused with 30-day notice if cash flow requires
- Faster vesting period (3 years vs. 4)

**Protections for Randy:**
- $15K upfront covers more of the actual build cost
- Monthly retainer provides baseline cash flow
- 8% app revenue share is uncapped — if the app becomes the primary ordering channel (which it should), this becomes the largest income component
- IP provisions same as Option A

**When this makes sense:** When Delivered values the growth engine but wants to maintain tighter cap table control, and has the cash flow to support a monthly retainer.

### Option C: "Full Stack Partner"

**Best for:** Maximum scope. Roviq becomes the full marketing/tech/strategy arm of Delivered. Appropriate if Delivered wants to aggressively pursue VC and statewide expansion.

| Component | Terms |
|---|---|
| **Upfront Fee** | $10,000 |
| **Equity** | 10-15% (vesting over 4 years, 1-year cliff) |
| **Revenue Share** | 3% of total gross revenue (no floor — starts from dollar one) |
| **Monthly Retainer** | $2,000/month for Months 1-6, scaling to $4,000/month for Months 7-12, then re-evaluated |
| **Roviq Role** | Co-founder level: CTO + CMO + Board advisor seat |
| **Additional** | Roviq participates in future fundraising rounds pro-rata (right to maintain ownership %) |

**The logic:** This is the "build a company together" option. Higher equity, lower rev share percentage but on ALL revenue (not just app), board participation, and pro-rata rights. This positions Roviq as a co-founder, not a service provider. The scaling retainer reflects increasing operational scope as the business grows.

**Revenue share trigger math:** At current estimated revenue (~$55-65K/month), 3% = $1,650-1,950/month. Not huge — but it starts immediately, and it grows with every dollar of revenue regardless of source. At $200K/month (Month 12 target), 3% = $6,000/month. Combined with the $4K retainer, that's $10K/month. Plus equity in a company doing $2.4M/year in a protected market — that equity has real value.

**Protections for Delivered:**
- Revenue share percentage is the lowest of all three options (3%)
- Retainer scales with the business — not front-loaded
- Board advisor seat is advisory, not a voting board seat — Delivered founders retain full control
- Vesting protects against early departure

**Protections for Randy:**
- Pro-rata rights protect against dilution in future funding rounds
- Board seat provides visibility into business decisions
- 3% on total revenue means the rev share can't be gamed by shifting orders between channels
- Co-founder framing supports the VC narrative and increases equity value

**When this makes sense:** When both parties are genuinely building together for a 3-5 year horizon and aiming for a VC raise or strategic exit.

### Recommended Structure

**Option A ("Startup Partner")** is the recommended starting point for negotiations. It offers the best balance of:
- Low cash burden on Delivered ($5K upfront, no retainer for 6 months)
- Meaningful equity for Roviq that reflects the true value contribution
- Clean revenue share structure that only activates when Delivered is winning
- Appropriate for the current stage of the business (pre-app, pre-expansion)

If Delivered pushes back on equity, Option B provides a cash-heavy alternative. If they want to go bigger and faster, Option C provides the full co-founder framework.

## 8. Risk Mitigation

### 8.1 What If the App Doesn't Drive Adoption?

**Risk level: Moderate**

**Mitigation strategies:**
- The app is not the only growth lever. Programmatic SEO, email/SMS, and the AC supply chain all drive value independently of app adoption.
- If app downloads are slow, shift marketing budget to web-based growth while iterating on app UX and value proposition.
- App-exclusive products and app-only discounts create concrete reasons to download beyond convenience.
- The app can be repositioned as a loyalty/rewards platform rather than the primary ordering channel if web ordering remains dominant.

**Worst case impact on deal:** Revenue share under Option B (app-only rev share) would be low. Under Options A and C (total revenue share), the impact is smaller because non-app growth still drives revenue. This is one reason Option A is recommended.

### 8.2 What If Statewide Expansion Is Slower Than Expected?

**Risk level: Moderate-Low**

**Mitigation strategies:**
- Expansion is digital-first. Programmatic SEO pages can be live in new markets months before any driver is hired. The cost of "testing" a market is essentially $0 — publish the pages, run targeted ads, see if orders come in.
- Central MA can continue to deepen. Even without expansion, there's meaningful growth potential in the existing 30-town footprint through better marketing, app adoption, and exclusive products.
- The projection model is conservative on new market ramp times (2-3 months to meaningful revenue). If a market ramps faster, projections are beaten.

**Worst case impact on deal:** Growth is slower but still positive. Equity value grows more slowly. Revenue share takes longer to become significant. None of these break the deal — they just extend the timeline.

### 8.3 What If Social Equity Exclusivity Gets Challenged?

**Risk level: Low (in the near term)**

The CCC voted 4-0 to maintain exclusivity through April 2029. This was a decisive, unanimous vote. Overturning it would require:
- A new CCC composition with different priorities (commissioners serve 5-year terms)
- Legislative action overriding the CCC (possible but politically difficult — social equity is popular)
- A successful legal challenge (no pending challenges as of February 2026)

**Mitigation strategies:**
- Even if exclusivity ends in 2027 or 2028 instead of 2029, Delivered would have 1-2 years of head start with an established app, customer base, and supply chain.
- The app, brand, and customer relationships are defensible even without regulatory protection. New entrants would still need to build everything from scratch.
- Vertical integration (AC + HOP) provides a cost advantage that isn't dependent on regulatory protection.

**Worst case impact on deal:** More competitors enter faster. But Delivered's head start (app, brand, supply chain, data) still provides meaningful advantages. First-mover advantage in cannabis is real — customers are habitual and switching costs (finding a new reliable source, re-entering preferences, losing loyalty points) are meaningful.

### 8.4 What Protects Randy If the Partnership Dissolves?

This is critical and must be addressed in the legal agreement:

**Vested equity remains.** Whatever equity has vested at the time of dissolution is Randy's permanently. Standard startup practice.

**IP reversion clause.** If the partnership dissolves before the 1-year cliff (meaning no equity has vested), the app intellectual property reverts to Roviq. Delivered receives a perpetual license to use the app as-is but cannot modify, rebrand, or resell it. This protects Randy from building a $100K+ asset for free.

**Revenue share survival.** Revenue share payments continue for 24 months after dissolution based on revenue from systems Randy built (app, SEO pages, email/SMS flows). This recognizes that the growth engine continues generating value after Randy's active involvement ends.

**Non-compete exception.** If the partnership dissolves due to Delivered's breach (defined in the agreement), Randy is free to offer similar services to competing delivery operators immediately. Otherwise, a 12-month non-compete for Massachusetts cannabis delivery specifically.

**Audit rights.** Randy retains the right to audit Delivered's revenue figures relevant to revenue share calculations for 36 months after dissolution.

**Upfront fee is non-refundable.** Regardless of outcome.

## 9. Why This Partnership, Why Now

### The App Gap Won't Last Forever

Zyp Run has $2.6 million in funding and a development team. Rolling Releaf has SERPCOM's technical resources behind them. Every month without an app is a month closer to someone else launching first. The competitive advantage of "first cannabis delivery app in Massachusetts" is available exactly once. After that, it's "another cannabis delivery app."

The difference between "first" and "second" in consumer apps is massive. The first app gets:
- Press coverage ("First cannabis delivery app launches in MA")
- Organic App Store discovery (novel category)
- Customer lock-in (once someone sets up an account, adds payment, earns loyalty points — they don't switch easily)

### First Mover in a Protected Market

This is the rare case where moving first doesn't mean taking a risk on an unproven market. The market exists ($20.4M and growing). The customers exist (600K+ cannabis consumers in MA). The regulatory protection exists (until 2029). The only question is whether Delivered captures a disproportionate share of that market — and the app is the weapon that enables it.

No other delivery operator in Massachusetts has:
- A marketing/technology partner with an existing cannabis app on the App Store
- A cultivator relationship that enables exclusive house-brand products
- A brand partner that provides app-exclusive offerings

This combination of **AC (supply) + HOP (brand) + Delivered (distribution) + Roviq (technology + marketing)** is a vertically integrated cannabis delivery company. That's not a marketing agency pitch. That's a business model.
