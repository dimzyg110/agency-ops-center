## 3. The app: first cannabis delivery app in Massachusetts

### React Native, iOS + Android from day one

This is a delivery utility app. It is not a cinematic brand experience. React Native gives us both platforms simultaneously. It eliminates the Android gap (Android exceeds 50% in urban/working-class demographics) and keeps development focused.

The architecture is straightforward. The Delivered app is the frontend. Blaze POS is the backend. Menu and inventory sync via Blaze's open API (`apidocs.blaze.me`) in real-time. Order placement and delivery tracking flow through Blaze. Blaze already handles dispatch with route optimization and driver mobile tools like navigation and ID verification. We don't rebuild those systems. We build the customer experience on top of them. That de-risks the technical build.

### Core features (V1 MVP)

The app handles ordering with a full product catalog synced via Blaze API. It includes smart search, favorites, and a cart with real-time inventory verification. Customers get live driver tracking pulled from Blaze's driver app. They also get dynamic ETAs and "5 minutes away" alerts. Push notifications cover order status and flash sales at $0.00 per send vs. $0.01-0.05 per SMS. The loyalty program runs points per dollar with 3-tier progression. It includes referral bonuses (give $10, get $10) and birthday rewards. Payment supports ACH, PIN-debit, and cash via Blaze's integrated processing. No credit cards, because cannabis can't use them. The app makes compliant payment feel painless.

### MA delivery compliance (935 CMR 500)

The app enforces compliance at every step. First-time customers complete ID verification during account creation. At the door, ID is scanned and must match the pre-verified customer. That customer must be physically present per 935 CMR 500. No roommate or neighbor handoffs. Delivery goes to private residences only. No hotels, dorms, public housing, or shelters. Every route generates a manifest tracked through Blaze with real-time driver position logging. Delivery windows auto-enforce per municipality. Purchase limits are enforced automatically per-customer, per-day.

All of this integrates with Blaze's backend. ID verification photos, digital signatures, and manifest tracking flow through one system.

### Scaling: what it actually takes

**Digital scaling (cheap, fast, no new license)**

Delivered's license (MD1303) covers statewide delivery. Expanding to new zones is a digital operation. It means coverage zone expansion into any municipality allowing adult-use retail. A programmatic SEO page per zone goes live automatically. No new license for new zones. Only CCC amendments for new warehouse locations. Cost is near-zero per new zone.

**Physical scaling (the real bottleneck)**

Warehouses: Currently 1 warehouse (Clinton, 75 Green St). This serves a ~60 min delivery radius efficiently. Boston, Western MA, and the Cape each need additional warehouses. A new warehouse means a CCC location amendment on the existing license. It is not a new application. Cost: $3-8K/month lease plus security buildout (cameras, vault, access control per 935 CMR 500).

Vehicles must be unmarked per 935 CMR 500. GPS tracking and real-time manifests are required. Cannabis transport insurance runs higher premiums. All-in cost is roughly $500-800/month per vehicle.

Drivers must complete CCC Delivery Core Curriculum before their first delivery. Background checks and CCC agent registration are required. Body cameras, ID verification photos, and digital signatures are required per 935 CMR 500. One order per consumer per trip per day. Pay runs $18-25/hour plus benefits in MA.

**Scaling phases**

Phase 1 (Months 1-6): Densify Central MA. Expand from ~30 to 50-60 towns with existing infrastructure. Digital only. No new ops costs. Phase 2 (Months 6-9): Hub to Boston. Second warehouse (metro west) and 2-3 new drivers. Cost: ~$11-16K/month additional. Phase 3 (Months 9-18): Statewide network with VC capital. 3-4 warehouses and 10-15 drivers. $40-60K/month ops burn. $200K+/month revenue target.

The license is the moat. Scaling is operational, not regulatory. The real limiter is capital for warehouses and drivers. That's what the Seed round funds.
