**Reviewer:** Senior Reviewer (Roviq QA)  
**Date:** February 10, 2026  
**Reports Reviewed:** 5 (website-audit, social-audit, seo-serpcom-audit, competitive-zyprun-audit, growth-opportunities)  
**Review Model:** claude-opus-4-6-thinking  

---

## Overall Assessment

This research package is **substantive but uneven**. The website audit and social audit are the strongest deliverables — detailed, evidence-grounded, and immediately actionable. The SEO/SERPCOM audit partially answers Randy's core question but stops short of the specifics needed to be convincing. The Zyp Run audit is solid on company profile but hedges too much on the tech play question with qualifiers rather than hard evidence. The growth opportunities report is the weakest — it makes bold revenue claims without sourcing them, invents specific numbers that appear fabricated (e.g., "30% cart abandonment at ID verification," "98% push notification open rate"), and risks embarrassing Roviq if a savvy client pushes back. Across all five reports, the Advanced Cultivators partnership angle is mentioned but never researched with any depth — no one actually investigated AC as a company. The package is **close to presentable** but needs targeted fixes before it reaches Randy's desk.

## Report-by-Report Review

### 1. Website Audit (`website-audit.md`)

**Grade: A-**

**Top Strength:** Exceptionally thorough. The sitemap analysis, spam page discovery, conversion path walkthrough, and competitive comparison table are all concrete and evidence-based. The discovery of the hacked casino pages in the sitemap is a genuine "holy shit" finding that gives Roviq immediate credibility in a pitch — we found something their current agency missed. The estimated effort/impact table at the end is exactly what Randy needs to price a proposal.

**Critical Gap:** The competitive comparison section covers Rolling Releaf and Zyp Run but doesn't mention **Major Bloom** — which is arguably The Delivered Inc's most direct competitor since Major Bloom is in Worcester (Central MA) with both a storefront AND delivery. The social audit and Zyp Run audit both flag Major Bloom, but this report skips them. For a "comprehensive website audit," that's a miss.

**Fix Needed:** Add a brief Major Bloom website comparison (even 5 bullet points) to the competitive section. Also: the "Estimated Effort to Fix" table quotes hours/days but not dollar ranges. Randy needs to translate these into a proposal — add approximate cost ranges or at minimum note "pricing TBD per scope."

### 2. Social Media Audit (`social-audit.md`)

**Grade: A-**

**Top Strength:** The follow-to-follower ratio analysis (2,443 following / 1,123 followers = 2.17:1 red flag) is the kind of specific, data-backed insight that makes Roviq look sharp. The 90-day content strategy with weekly phases is actionable and ready to drop into a proposal. The multilingual content gap (English/Spanish/Portuguese) is a brilliant catch — this is a real differentiator that Delivered isn't leveraging at all on social.

**Critical Gap:** Engagement estimates are honest about being estimates ("~15-40 likes/post"), but the report never attempts to verify even a few real posts. The agent could have searched for specific Instagram posts indexed by Google or checked publicly visible engagement on recent Reels. The "estimated local/relevant followers: 30-40% at best" figure has no methodology behind it — it's a guess presented as analysis.

**Fix Needed:** Either verify 3-5 actual post engagement numbers (via search-indexed Instagram content or web scraping) or explicitly caveat the estimates section with "requires Meta Business Suite access for verification." The 30-40% local follower estimate should either be removed or reframed as "industry benchmark for accounts using follow-for-follow tactics" with a citation.

### 3. SEO + SERPCOM Audit (`seo-serpcom-audit.md`)

**Grade: B-**

**Top Strength:** The report correctly identifies SERPCOM as an SEO-first agency, finds their Rolling Releaf project page, and connects the dots to a programmatic local SEO + catalog SEO strategy. The back-of-envelope traffic calculation (if $250K/mo → ~2,300-2,900 orders → 40K-100K visits/month) is the right framework for making the revenue claim tangible.

**Critical Gap:** This is Randy's #1 question — "A competitor uses SERPCOM and makes $250K/month — who is SERPCOM, what are they doing?" — and the report **doesn't fully answer it**. We get that SERPCOM does "programmatic local SEO" and has a "Prometheus Publishing Platform," but:
- **No detail on what the Prometheus platform actually does.** Is it auto-generating pages? Content spinning? AI content? The report just quotes SERPCOM's own marketing copy.
- **No verification of the $250K/month claim.** The report reverse-engineers what traffic would be needed, but never questions whether $250K/mo is even plausible for Rolling Releaf. Is this a number Randy heard from someone? Is it public? Is Rolling Releaf really doing $3M/year as a delivery-only operator in Boston?
- **No analysis of Rolling Releaf's actual search rankings.** The report says "expect mid-SERP positions" for Delivered but never checks where Rolling Releaf actually ranks for "weed delivery Boston" or similar terms. If SERPCOM is supposedly driving $250K/mo, we should see Rolling Releaf dominating those SERPs — did the agent check?
- **"GBP: Not verified"** appears multiple times. Google Business Profile is foundational for local SEO. Not verifying it is a significant omission.

**Fix Needed:**
1. Actually search "weed delivery Boston" and "cannabis delivery Boston MA" and report where Rolling Releaf ranks (top 3? Page 1? Not found?). This directly validates or undermines the SERPCOM claim.
2. Research the Prometheus Publishing Platform more — check SERPCOM's blog, case studies, or LinkedIn for specifics.
3. Add a caveat on the $250K/month figure: "Source: client intel (unverified). Roviq has not independently confirmed Rolling Releaf's revenue."
4. Check Rolling Releaf's GBP (Google Maps search for "Rolling Releaf" or "cannabis delivery Newton MA").

### 4. Competitive / Zyp Run Audit (`competitive-zyprun-audit.md`)

**Grade: B**

**Top Strength:** The company profile is well-researched — founders named, funding details sourced, operational model described. The "Is it a tech play? No — primarily a regulatory moat + operator play" conclusion is analytically sound and directly answers Randy's question with a clear stance.

**Critical Gap:** The conclusion that Zyp Run is "not a tech play" is stated confidently but the **evidence is circumstantial**. The report says the platform "does not appear to be monetized as a SaaS or marketplace" — but how does the agent know? Did they check Zyp Run's LinkedIn for engineering hiring? Did they look at job postings suggesting platform expansion? Did they check if Zyp Run has filed any patents or published any tech-focused press? The reasoning is "they haven't expanded geographically, therefore it's not a tech play" — that's a correlation argument, not proof.

Additionally, the $2.6M seed round details are thin. The report names Satori Investment Partners and Mollitiam Capital but doesn't investigate these investors. Are they cannabis-focused? Tech-focused? This would directly inform whether the money was for tech or operations.

The "Other Known MA Delivery Operators" section (Part B) is notably incomplete — it lists a few operators then says "Action recommended: build a verified list from CCC." For a competitive landscape report, this is punting on a core deliverable.

**Fix Needed:**
1. Research Satori Investment Partners and Mollitiam Capital — even a 2-sentence profile of each investor would strengthen the tech-vs-ops argument.
2. Check Zyp Run's LinkedIn for team composition (how many engineers vs. ops/logistics staff?) — this directly indicates whether it's a tech company or an ops company.
3. Either complete the MA delivery operator list or remove the "Partial List" section and honestly state "a full CCC audit was out of scope for this sprint."

### 5. Growth Opportunities (`growth-opportunities.md`)

**Grade: C**

**Top Strength:** The AC (Advanced Cultivators) partnership section is the most specific treatment of this topic across all five reports. The "house brand" / white-label concept, exclusive drops strategy, and revenue synergy loop are concrete and pitch-ready ideas. The service fit section at the end correctly frames what Roviq would actually sell.

**Critical Gaps (Multiple):**

1. **Fabricated or unsourced statistics.** This report contains several numbers that appear invented:
   - "30% of first-time carts are abandoned" at ID verification — **where does this come from?** No source. No "estimated" qualifier. If Randy quotes this in a pitch and the client asks for the source, Roviq has nothing.
   - "98% open rate" for push notifications — this is a commonly cited marketing stat but it's from 2016-era blog posts and is widely debunked. Actual push notification open rates for retail apps are 5-15%. **This is a credibility-destroying number if presented to a sophisticated client.**
   - "$75k - $125k for a custom React Native wrapper" — based on what? No scoping, no feature list justification. This could be $40K or $200K depending on scope.
   - "+$100k - $250k Monthly Rev" from SEO — this literally mirrors the unverified competitor claim ($250K/mo) and presents it as Delivered's opportunity with zero market sizing to support it.

2. **No actual research on Advanced Cultivators.** The report positions AC as "the missing link" and a Roviq client but never investigates: What does AC grow? Where are they located? What's their current distribution? Are they already supplying Delivered? What's their production capacity? The "house brand" idea is creative but entirely hypothetical without knowing anything about AC's actual operation.

3. **The revenue opportunity table is aspirational, not analytical.** "+15% Gross Margin" from a white-label line, "+20% Retention/LTV" from an app — these are round numbers with no model behind them. They read like pitch deck optimism, not research.

4. **Dismissive of social media.** The report says Delivered doesn't need "generic social media lifestyle posts" but needs "conversion-focused digital infrastructure." This directly contradicts the social audit's findings and recommendations. Social is important for a delivery brand with no storefront — the growth report shouldn't dismiss it.

**Fix Needed:**
1. **Remove or source every specific statistic.** The 30% cart abandonment, 98% push rate, and revenue projections all need either citations or "estimated" qualifiers with methodology.
2. **Actually research Advanced Cultivators** — at minimum: location, license type, product portfolio, current retail/wholesale partners. Without this, the AC strategy section is fiction.
3. **Tone down the revenue opportunity table** — reframe as "potential impact ranges" with assumptions stated, not as promised outcomes.
4. **Remove the dismissal of social media** or reframe as "social alone is insufficient without conversion infrastructure" (which is a defensible point).

## Final Verdict

### Ready to Present? **Yes, with fixes.**

The package has real substance and several "wow" findings (spam pages, follow-for-follow ratio, multilingual gap, Canva logo filename). But the growth opportunities report has credibility risks, and the AC gap is a blind spot Randy will immediately notice.

### Priority Fixes Before Delivery

1. **Research Advanced Cultivators immediately.** At minimum: location, license type, what they grow, current distribution partners, any existing relationship with Delivered. This was Randy's explicit ask and zero of five reports delivered on it.

2. **Remove or source all fabricated statistics in growth-opportunities.md.** The "98% push notification open rate" and "30% cart abandonment at ID verification" are indefensible. Replace with sourced or honestly estimated figures.

3. **Verify Rolling Releaf's search rankings for key Boston delivery terms.** One Google search for "weed delivery Boston" would tell us if SERPCOM's work is actually ranking. This directly strengthens or weakens the SERPCOM story.

4. **Reconcile the app recommendation across reports.** Either align on "lightweight MVP first" or "custom build" — not both. Add a brief evaluation of existing white-label options (Dutchie app, Jane, etc.).

5. **Add a revenue estimate for Delivered's current baseline.** The growth report estimates AOV and frequency but never completes the math. Even a rough range ($X-$Y/month) gives Randy something to anchor the ROI conversation.

6. **Resolve the social media contradiction.** The growth report dismissing social while the social audit builds a full 90-day strategy sends mixed signals. Align the message: social matters for brand/awareness, conversion infrastructure matters for revenue — both are needed.

7. **Add source links for market data claims.** The $1.64B MA market, $15M delivery segment, and $2.6M Zyp Run raise all need linked sources. "Reported" is not a citation.

8. **Clarify the blog/content subdomain situation.** The main blog (/blog/) is active and posting regularly. The content.thedeliveredinc.com subdomain is dead. These are different things and the reports blur them.
