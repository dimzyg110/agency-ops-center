## STEP 4 — CONVERSION PATH ANALYSIS

### 4.1 Path: Landing Page → Order Placed

```
1. Land on homepage (or local SEO page like /worcesters-premier-cannabis-home-delivery/)
2. Age verification gate (must confirm 21+)
3. Click "Shop Now" or browse category cards
4. Arrive at Dutchie-powered menu (/menu/)
5. Browse/filter products (by type, brand, tier, effect)
6. Add items to cart
7. Click cart icon (top right)
8. Create account (first time) — requires ID upload, contact info
9. Select delivery window (hourly starting 12pm)
10. Select payment method (ACH / debit / cash)
11. Click "Pay" to submit order
12. Receive text confirmation
13. Receive text updates (order status, driver en route)
14. Driver texts 5 min before arrival, calls on arrival
15. ID verification at door + signature
16. Post-delivery survey sent
```

### 4.2 Friction Points

1. **Age gate** — Necessary for compliance but adds a click before anything else. No way around this.
2. **No address check upfront** — User can browse the entire menu, fill their cart, and only discover at checkout that their town isn't in the delivery zone or that they don't meet the minimum. This is a *major* friction point. The delivery minimums range from $25 to $150 — and recently Marlborough went up to $150 with a note displayed at the top of the menu page. There's no smart address-based minimum display.
3. **Account creation wall** — First-time customers must create an account AND upload ID before ordering. Necessary for compliance, but there's no "guest checkout" path. ID upload can be frustrating on mobile.
4. **Payment complexity** — Three payment options (ACH, debit, cash) with a $3.50 fee for debit. ACH is unfamiliar to many consumers. This creates decision friction and confusion.
5. **No "delivery ETA" display** — You choose a delivery window but there's no real-time tracking or estimated arrival until text updates begin. Modern delivery expectations (DoorDash, UberEats) include live tracking.
6. **Minimum order notice** — The Marlborough minimum increase ($150) is displayed as a text banner at the top of the menu page. This is jarring and not context-sensitive. Other towns' minimums are only found on the Zones page.
7. **Menu is Dutchie-standard** — The menu works but feels like every other Dutchie-powered dispensary. No custom filtering, no "staff picks" section, no "best sellers" callout on the menu itself.

### 4.3 Lead Capture Mechanisms

**Current lead capture: Minimal.**

- **Text/SMS program** — Referenced in press materials ("sign up for their texting program") but no visible signup CTA or form on the website
- **Contact form** — Generic form on `/contact/` page
- **Account creation** — Captures user data but only at checkout, not for pre-purchase marketing
- **No email signup** anywhere
- **No popup or banner offers** for first-time visitors
- **No exit-intent capture**
- **No loyalty/rewards program** visible

**This is the biggest gap on the site.** A delivery business lives and dies on repeat orders. Without email/SMS capture outside of the checkout flow, they're leaving massive remarketing potential on the table.

### 4.4 Post-Order Experience

Based on their descriptions:
- ✅ Text updates throughout delivery
- ✅ Driver texts 5 min before arrival + calls on arrival
- ✅ ID verification at door (compliance)
- ✅ Post-delivery survey
- ✅ Body camera surveillance (compliance)
- ❓ No mention of follow-up marketing (reorder reminders, new product alerts)
- ❓ No mention of referral program
- ❓ No mention of loyalty points

The post-order experience sounds solid from a service perspective but lacks marketing automation. They're not leveraging post-purchase touchpoints to drive repeat orders.

## STEP 6 — VERDICT

### Overall Grade: **C+**

The site is functional and operationally sound, but it's a Minimum Viable Website for a business with real growth potential. The operational experience (delivery process, brand partnerships, community integrations) sounds better than the website makes it look.

---

### Top 3 Things Working

1. **Local SEO Strategy is Strong** — 19 town-specific landing pages, active blog (2-3 posts/week), Dutchie Plus sub-path menu for product-level SEO. They're building the organic foundation. This is likely CannaPlanners' doing and it's the right play.

2. **Tier System (Exotics / OGs / Budget Buds)** — Smart product differentiation that no competitor is doing. Allows customers to self-select by budget/preference. This is a genuine competitive advantage that should be featured more prominently.

3. **Brand Partnership Content** — The `/partnered-brands/` page with detailed brand stories is excellent content marketing. It builds trust, supports SEO, differentiates from competitors who just list products, and strengthens vendor relationships. The small business cross-promotions (Dragon's Leyr, Green Zone, etc.) show community roots.

---

### Top 3 Things Hurting Them

1. **⚠️ Hacked Spam Pages in Sitemap** — 5 casino/gambling spam pages are indexed in the XML sitemap from a 2022-2023 WordPress compromise. This is actively hurting their domain authority and could trigger Google penalties. The dead `content.thedeliveredinc.com` subdomain (503 error) is also a mess. **This needs to be fixed immediately.**

2. **Zero Lead Capture / No Remarketing Infrastructure** — No email signup, no SMS opt-in form (despite having a texting program), no first-time visitor offer, no loyalty program, no referral incentive. For a delivery business where repeat orders are everything, this is leaving revenue on the table every single day. Compare to Zyp Run's free first delivery + $100 referral program.

3. **Generic Visual Identity & Thin Trust Signals** — The site looks like every other CannaPlanners template. The logo image is literally named "Untitled-design-9.png" (Canva). The About page is one paragraph with no team photos. No customer reviews anywhere on the site. No "Top 3 Best Dispensaries in Central MA" badge displayed (despite claiming this in SEO copy). The site doesn't convey the brand personality that their community partnerships and delivery experience suggest exists.

---

### Biggest Missed Opportunity

**SMS/Email Marketing Capture + Loyalty/Rewards Program**

Delivered has the operational infrastructure (they already text customers, they already have a texting program, they already have a customer survey). But they have **zero front-end lead capture** on the website. 

Here's the math: If the site gets even 500 unique visitors per month, and they captured 10% into an SMS list, that's 50 new SMS subscribers/month × 12 months = 600 subscribers. At even a 5% conversion rate on weekly text blasts, that's 30 incremental orders per week. At a $75 average order value, that's **$2,250/week or ~$117,000/year** in attributable revenue from a simple SMS popup and weekly text.

They're also missing a loyalty program (Zyp Run has referrals, Rolling Releaf has brand loyalty). A simple "order 5 times, get $10 off" punch-card approach would drive repeat behavior. Combined with the existing text infrastructure, this is the highest-ROI move they can make.

### Roviq Assessment

Delivered Inc has a legitimate competitive moat: they own Central MA cannabis delivery with no direct competitors, they have the regulatory exclusivity window extended through 2026+, they have strong brand partnerships, and they've built a solid SEO foundation. The operational side of the business appears sound.

The website is their weakest link. It's a template site that doesn't match the quality of the actual customer experience. The security issues (spam pages) are embarrassing and need immediate attention. The lack of any lead capture or retention marketing infrastructure is the single biggest revenue leak.

**This is a business that's doing $X in revenue despite their website, not because of it.** A strategic digital investment would unlock significant growth — not from rebuilding from scratch, but from fixing the fundamentals: security cleanup, lead capture, trust signals, and a retention layer. The ROI case is straightforward.
