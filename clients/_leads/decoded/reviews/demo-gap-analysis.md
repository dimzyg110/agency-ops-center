# DECODED Demo Gap Analysis
**Date:** 2026-02-10  
**Analyst:** Strategic UX/Brand Reviewer  
**Demo URL:** https://roviq-ai.github.io/decoded-demo/  
**Client Site:** https://decodedshop.myshopify.com/

---

## Executive Summary

The demo nails the **visual language of luxury streetwear** but misses the **soul of Decoded**. Our mock product names sound like a Loro Piana diffusion line. Decoded's actual products read like Travis Scott merch meets 90s Hot Topic. This is not a bug. It's the brand. The disconnect is significant enough that showing this demo as-is could hurt the deal.

**Verdict:** The interaction design is sellable. The content strategy needs immediate correction before any client meeting.

---

## 1. What the Demo Gets RIGHT

### Visual Direction (A-)
The matte gold (#C9A84C) against charcoal (#0D0D0D) is pitch-perfect for "Decoded Noir." The film grain overlay, heavy 900-weight typography, and countdown mechanics create legitimate urgency. This aesthetic direction aligns with the luxury sub-brand positioning on their actual site.

### Interaction Model (A)
Vertical scroll-snap cards are the correct format for Decoded's audience. With 20.4K Instagram followers and 0.27% engagement (below the 1-3% healthy range), they need content formats that boost interaction. TikTok-style feeds force engagement through passive scrolling. This addresses a real business problem.

### Mobile-First Execution (A)
100dvh sizing and touch-optimized reactions (fire/skull buttons) show understanding of the streetwear consumer's primary shopping context. Decoded's current Shopify Dawn theme is desktop-biased and generic. The demo fixes this.

### The "Drop" Mechanics (B+)
The countdown timer to "THE PHANTOM COLLECTION" mirrors how Decoded's "Decoded Noir" collection is positioned as exclusive. The scarcity framework (gold dot indicators, "ADD TO VAULT" language) maps correctly to their luxury sub-brand narrative.

## 2. What the Demo Gets WRONG (Critical Issues)

### Product Naming: Complete Disconnect

| Demo Mock Names | Actual Decoded Names |
|-----------------|----------------------|
| Midnight Bomber | Lone Wolf Hoodie |
| Phantom Hoodie | DAWG Hoodie |
| Ghost Line Tee | SINNERS TALK |
| Vault Lock Cap | Stop Soliciting Tee |
| Noir Runner | Broke Tee |
| The Confession | 911 Turbo Shirt |

**The Problem:** Our demo uses vague luxury descriptors (Midnight, Phantom, Ghost, Vault) that sound like every other elevated streetwear brand. Decoded uses specific, polarizing, culturally-coded names:
- **"DAWG"** — references their podcast/community language
- **"911 Turbo"** — automotive culture embedded in brand DNA
- **"SINNERS TALK"** / **"Kingdom has Fallen"** — religious/moral tension themes
- **"Broke Tee"** — ironic self-awareness about spending $125 on a t-shirt
- **"Stop Soliciting"** — anti-establishment edge

**Fix:** Every mock product name needs replacement with actual Decoded products or names that match their tonal formula: [Cultural Reference] + [Streetwear Garment]. Examples that would fit: "Turbo Velocity Hoodie," "Noir Saints Tee," "806 Legacy Bomber."

### Pricing Reality Check

| Demo Prices | Actual Price Range |
|-------------|-------------------|
| $55 (cap) | $25-45 (tees) |
| $85 (tee) | $45-65 (graphic tees) |
| $145 (light jacket) | $80-120 (hoodies/zip-ups) |
| $165 (hoodie) | $100-150 (premium tops/sets) |
| $220 (sneakers) | — |
| $285 (bomber) | — |

**The Problem:** Demo prices are 60-90% higher than Decoded's actual products. The "Noir Runner" at $220 suggests we're adding footwear they don't sell. The "Midnight Bomber" at $285 implies a price point they've never tested.

**Fix:** Adjust all demo prices to match actual Decoded pricing tiers:
- Tees: $45-65
- Hoodies/Zip-ups: $80-120
- Premium/Sets: $120-150

This isn't about underpricing. It's about credibility. Showing a $285 bomber to a brand whose highest SKU is $150 suggests we didn't research them.

### Missing Brand Pillars

**806 The Show (Podcast)** — Decoded is not just apparel. It's a media brand. The demo has no audio integration, no podcast references, no "tune in" CTAs. Their community is built around the show. Ignoring it is ignoring their primary audience engagement tool.

**Automotive × Streetwear Fusion** — The 911 Turbo, Velocity, Desperado, and Motor Cross products prove cars are core to their visual language. The demo has no automotive visual references (no tire textures, no carbon fiber patterns, no racing stripe motifs).

**Youth/Community Angle** — DAWG Youth Athletic Tee ($25) shows they serve younger demographics. The demo's "luxury" positioning skews too adult, too serious.

## 4. Photography Direction (Nano Banana Pro AI Prompts)

### Shot Types by Product Category

**Hoodies/Zip-ups (Lone Wolf, Homeless Rich, Decoded Angels):**
- **Style:** Editorial street photography
- **Model:** Male, 20-30, athletic build, neutral expression with edge
- **Setting:** Urban environment — concrete walls, parking garages, street corners at dusk
- **Lighting:** Low-key, single source from side, dramatic shadows
- **Pose:** Natural movement — adjusting hood, hands in pockets, mid-stride
- **Prompt formula:** "Editorial streetwear photography, male model wearing [product], urban parking garage at golden hour, dramatic side lighting, matte black and gold color palette, shot on 35mm film, grain texture, high contrast, cinematic composition, waist-up framing, serious expression, luxury streetwear aesthetic, 4K detail"

**Graphic Tees (Hand of God, SINNERS TALK, Broke Tee):**
- **Style:** Close-up product + model hybrid
- **Focus:** Chest graphic clearly visible
- **Model:** Diverse casting, direct eye contact with camera
- **Setting:** Simple textured backgrounds (brick, concrete, metal)
- **Prompt formula:** "Streetwear product photography, model wearing [product], close-up chest-level shot, graphic tee clearly visible, urban brick wall background, natural daylight with fill, shallow depth of field, film grain, muted color palette with gold accents, attitude-forward pose, contemporary streetwear editorial style"

**Sets/Coordinates (Noir Strata):**
- **Style:** Full-body editorial
- **Model:** Tall, slim, commanding presence
- **Setting:** Minimalist architectural — white concrete stairs, modern building facades
- **Pose:** Standing still, direct to camera, powerful stance
- **Prompt formula:** "Luxury streetwear editorial, full body shot, model wearing matching [product set], minimalist architectural background, overcast lighting, matte black fabric texture, gold hardware details, standing pose facing camera, high fashion composition, neutral expression, premium aesthetic, sharp focus, 8K detail"

**Automotive-Themed (911 Turbo, Velocity, Desperado):**
- **Style:** Lifestyle/action hybrid
- **Setting:** Car culture environments — garages, racetrack pits, highway overpasses
- **Props:** Vintage sports car (Porsche 911 silhouette if possible), racing gloves, sunglasses
- **Prompt formula:** "Automotive streetwear lifestyle photography, model wearing [product] leaning against vintage sports car, urban racetrack environment, late afternoon lighting, lens flare, carbon fiber and leather textures in frame, cinematic color grading, gold and black dominant palette, confident posture, car culture aesthetic, 4K detail"

### Technical Specifications for AI Generation

- **Aspect ratio:** 9:16 (vertical) for TikTok feed compatibility
- **Resolution:** Minimum 1080x1920, upscale to 4K for retina displays
- **Consistency:** Same model face across all shots (use seed locking or consistent character prompts)
- **Background separation:** Ensure subjects are clearly separable from backgrounds for potential cutout/recomposition

## 5. UI/UX Gaps for Client Meeting

### Missing Conversion Elements

1. **Size Selection** — Currently buried or absent. Add inline size pills (S/M/L/XL/XXL) that highlight on tap.

2. **Color Swatches** — Decoded products have multiple variants. Show Black/White/Tan options as tappable circles.

3. **Quick Add vs. View Details** — Current "ADD TO VAULT" implies instant purchase. Add a secondary "VIEW FULL DROP" CTA for consideration-stage shoppers.

4. **Social Proof** — 0.27% engagement is low. The demo should show "806 listeners bought this" or "Limited to 50" to create legitimate scarcity (they do limited drops).

5. **Audio Integration** — Since 806 The Show is core to the brand, add a "Listen While You Browse" sticky audio player with latest episode. This differentiates from standard e-commerce.

### Mobile-Specific Improvements

1. **Thumb Zone Optimization** — "ADD TO VAULT" button is likely too low. Move primary CTA to bottom 25% of screen.

2. **Swipe Gestures** — Add left-swipe for "Save to Wishlist," right-swipe for "Share." Matches TikTok interaction patterns.

3. **Haptic Feedback** — Mention haptic on fire/skull reactions. Adds tactile luxury feel.

### Post-Purchase Loop (Demo Preview)

Add a mock "Order Confirmed" state that shows:
- "Your drop is secured"
- "Tune into 806 The Show this Thursday for early access to next drop"
- Instagram share CTA

This demonstrates understanding of their community flywheel.

## 6. Competitive Positioning for the Pitch

### What to Say

> "Your current Shopify site treats Decoded like a generic apparel brand. The 0.27% engagement rate tells the story — flat-lay photography on white backgrounds doesn't sell streetwear. Supreme doesn't do flat-lays. Kith doesn't do flat-lays. Your automotive × streetwear positioning demands editorial photography with models, movement, and attitude.
>
> This TikTok-style feed format addresses your engagement problem directly. It forces interaction through scroll mechanics. The fire/skull reactions give you qualitative data on product heat before you even manufacture. The countdown timers create urgency that converts browsers to buyers.
>
> We've selected your highest-margin products for the initial feed — Noir Strata, Hand of God, All Glory. These are your statement pieces. They deserve statement presentation."

### What NOT to Say

- Don't mention the mock product names or prices in the current demo
- Don't position this as "luxury" without acknowledging their accessible price points
- Don't ignore 806 The Show — ask about integration opportunities

## 8. Final Recommendation

**Do not show the current demo to Decoded without modifications.** The product names and pricing create a credibility gap that will undermine the entire pitch. They'll see a generic luxury mockup, not their brand elevated.

**The interaction design is the sell.** The scroll-snap cards, reaction buttons, countdown mechanics, and visual polish are genuinely impressive. But the content strategy must match the brand's actual voice: automotive-obsessed, podcast-driven, youth-culture-connected, religiously-coded, and self-aware about the irony of premium streetwear pricing.

Fix the names. Fix the prices. Add the 806 references. Then you have a demo that makes the owner say "I need this."
