# Cookies & STIIIZY: Cannabis Social Media & Branding Analysis

*Research compiled: February 2026*

---

## Executive Summary

Cookies and STIIIZY represent two distinct but highly successful approaches to cannabis branding. **Cookies** built its empire on hip-hop culture integration, streetwear crossover, and founder-driven authenticity. **STIIIZY** achieved dominance through product innovation, minimalist tech-forward branding, and aggressive vertical integration. Both offer valuable lessons for cannabis social media strategy.

---

## 🍪 COOKIES

### Visual Style

**Core Aesthetic:**
- **Signature blue** (iconic "Cookies blue") as primary brand color
- Stylized "C" logo instantly recognizable
- **Streetwear-meets-cannabis** design language
- Bold, graphic designs combining cannabis culture with urban/retro aesthetics
- Blue drawstrings on hoodies became trademark identifier

**Instagram Grid Style:**
- Mix of product shots, lifestyle content, and music/culture moments
- Behind-the-scenes cultivation footage
- Heavy founder (Berner) presence throughout
- Consistent blue brand color integration
- User-generated content featuring Cookies apparel

**Key Visual Elements:**
- Jarred flower with premium presentation
- Dispensary retail experiences (lines outside stores, grand openings)
- Artists/celebrities wearing Cookies gear
- Cultivation facility tours and strain close-ups

### Content Mix

**Lifestyle (40%):**
- Music video clips featuring Berner
- Celebrity endorsements (organic, not paid-feeling)
- Festival/event coverage
- Dispensary culture and community moments

**Product (30%):**
- Strain highlights and new drops
- "Inside the Bag" strain selection content
- Premium packaging photography
- Limited edition releases

**Culture (30%):**
- Hip-hop crossover content
- Streetwear collaborations (New Era, PSD)
- Cannabis advocacy and industry commentary
- "Marijuana Mania" documentary-style content
- Cannabis Cup appearances and judging

### Compliance Approach

**Trademark Workaround Strategy:**
Cookies pioneered a critical compliance innovation. Since cannabis can't be federally trademarked, Berner created **Cookies SF as a streetwear line first**. This allowed them to:
- Trademark the logo and branding through apparel
- Build brand equity in non-cannabis spaces
- Sell merchandise in mainstream retailers (Zumiez, Lids) without age restrictions
- Create visibility before dispensary openings

**Social Media Compliance:**
- Focus on lifestyle/culture content over explicit product sales
- Heavy emphasis on apparel and accessories
- Music career provides "parallel track" content that isn't cannabis-direct
- Use of YouTube's Couch Lock Network for long-form cannabis content
- Educational/documentary style bypasses some restrictions

**Regulatory Navigation:**
- Partner-based expansion model for state-by-state licensing
- Medical cannabis emphasis in restricted markets
- International expansion through licensed partners (e.g., Mamedica in UK)

### Celebrity/Culture Integration

**This is Cookies' superpower.** Unlike brands that pay for celebrity endorsements, Cookies' celebrity relationships are organic and reciprocal:

**Key Partnerships:**
- **Wiz Khalifa** - Early advocate who tried GSC and became vocal supporter; signed Berner to Taylor Gang
- **Rick Ross** - "Collins Ave" strain collaboration
- **Run The Jewels** - "Ooh La La" strain partnership with LEMONNADE sub-brand
- **Chris Brown** - Featured in "Yoko" video where Berner wore Cookies hoodie

**Integration Strategy:**
- Berner IS the celebrity—founder-as-influencer model
- Collaborations feel like peer partnerships, not paid placements
- Artists feature Cookies organically because they actually use it
- Strain naming often tied to collaborations
- Cross-promotion through music videos, not ads

**Authenticity Markers:**
- Berner's pre-Cookies history (High Times Cannabis Cup judging, budtender experience)
- Genuine cultivation expertise (partnership with Jai "Jigga" Chang)
- 150+ strain library demonstrates depth, not just marketing

### Streetwear/Fashion Crossover

**Revenue Reality:**
Cookies clothing did **$50+ million in sales** in 2021 alone—making apparel a major business, not just merchandise.

**Key Partnerships:**
- **New Era** - Caps and headwear in mainstream retail
- **PSD** - Underwear collaboration
- **Zumiez** - Distribution through national chain
- **Lids.ca** - Canadian streetwear retail

**Strategic Value:**
- Apparel available to under-21 consumers in non-age-gated stores
- Brand visibility in malls/streetwear shops builds awareness before consumers can legally purchase cannabis
- Clothing signals identity: "I smoke lots of weed, but in the cool Wiz Khalifa way, not the lame suburban-mom way" (NYT)
- Creates secondary revenue stream independent of cannabis regulations
- Enables trademarking and brand protection

**Brand Positioning:**
Often compared to **Supreme** for creating cultural cachet and limited-drop hype around cannabis culture.

### Video/Reels Strategy

**YouTube Dominance:**
- **Couch Lock Network** - Cookies' media arm for long-form content
- "Marijuana Mania" - Documentary series exploring global cannabis culture
- "Inside the Bag" - Strain selection process (educational + product showcase)
- Berner's music videos double as brand content

**Content Formats:**
- Documentary-style cultivation footage
- Dispensary opening coverage with fan lines
- Artist collaborations and studio sessions
- Strain reviews and genetic explanations
- Behind-the-scenes at events/concerts

**Platform Strategy:**
- YouTube for long-form (less restricted than Instagram)
- Instagram for lifestyle/culture moments
- Music video integration (embedded product placement)
- Organic artist posts extend reach without ad spend

## 🔋 STIIIZY

### Visual Style

**Core Aesthetic:**
- **Minimalist, tech-forward** design language
- Clean sans-serif typography
- Black/white/monochrome base with vibrant accent colors
- Sleek hardware design as brand centerpiece
- Apple-like product photography

**Instagram Grid Style:**
- Product-forward with clean backgrounds
- High-contrast, high-energy imagery
- Event/community photos
- "For the culture" messaging
- Less founder-dependent than Cookies

**Key Visual Elements:**
- Pod system hardware as iconic product
- Lifestyle shots of young consumers (controversial—led to lawsuits)
- Dispensary retail environments
- Concert/event sponsorship visuals

### Content Mix

**Product (45%):**
- Hardware innovation (pods, batteries, All-in-One vapes)
- New strain/product drops
- Limited edition releases
- "40's" pre-roll multipacks
- Concentrates and edibles

**Lifestyle (35%):**
- Event sponsorships
- Community engagement
- User-generated content
- "For the culture" positioning

**Culture (20%):**
- Unity and diversity messaging
- Community initiatives (STIIIZY Joint Efforts)
- Industry awards and recognition
- Social responsibility content

### Compliance Approach

**The STIIIZY Controversy:**
STIIIZY has faced significant legal challenges around compliance:
- **2024 lawsuit** alleged marketing to minors through "vibrant colors, pulsating music, and social media ads featuring young people"
- Products appeared in unlicensed markets (bodegas, smoke shops in NY)
- LA Times investigation connected co-founder to properties leasing to unlicensed dispensaries
- New York regulators conducted surprise audit in 2025

**Social Media Challenges:**
- Platform restrictions led to age-gating requirements
- Content that felt "too youth-appealing" drew regulatory scrutiny
- Had to balance street credibility with compliance optics

**What They Do Right:**
- Strong age verification on owned properties
- Vertically integrated operations provide supply chain control
- Dispensary-focused marketing (less reliant on social)
- Robust compliance infrastructure after controversies

**The Paradox:**
STIIIZY's "outlaw reputation" from controversies has paradoxically *helped* its street credibility. As Forbes noted: "As a legacy brand, the added street cred has burnished the company's position as the industry's leader. Stiiizy is for stoners, not soccer moms."

### Celebrity/Culture Integration

**Different Model Than Cookies:**
STIIIZY relies less on celebrity partnerships and more on:
- **Community/culture positioning** - "For the culture" tagline
- **Event presence** - Concerts, festivals, community events
- **Legacy brand credibility** - Pre-legalization origins
- **Word-of-mouth** from dedicated consumer base

**Founder Role:**
- CEO James Kim is visible but not celebrity-level
- Less personal brand than Berner—company-first, not founder-first
- Military veteran narrative adds authenticity
- "Swap meet hustle" origin story resonates

### Streetwear/Fashion Crossover

**Limited Fashion Play:**
Unlike Cookies, STIIIZY hasn't pursued aggressive apparel strategy:
- Merchandise exists but isn't a major revenue driver
- No major streetwear partnerships (no New Era equivalent)
- Brand visibility comes from product ubiquity, not clothing

**Why It Works Anyway:**
- STIIIZY achieved scale through product dominance (#1 selling brand nationally)
- Hardware design is fashion-adjacent (sleek pen design)
- 1 in 8 cannabis products sold in US is STIIIZY—doesn't need apparel awareness

### Video/Reels Strategy

**Content Approach:**
- Product-focused Reels showing hardware
- Behind-the-scenes cultivation/manufacturing
- Community event coverage
- Award announcements
- New market launch content

**Less Reliant on Video:**
- STIIIZY's scale came from retail footprint (50+ stores) rather than content
- Product speaks for itself—less storytelling needed
- YouTube not as central as for Cookies

## Key Takeaways for Cannabis Social Media Strategy

### From Cookies:
1. **Build parallel revenue streams** (apparel) for brand visibility and trademark protection
2. **Founder-as-influencer** can be more authentic than paid celebrity
3. **Music/culture integration** feels organic when genuinely embedded
4. **Documentary content** on YouTube bypasses Instagram restrictions
5. **Streetwear partnerships** reach consumers before they're legal-age buyers
6. **Strain collaborations** with artists create shared cultural moments

### From STIIIZY:
1. **Product innovation** can drive brand without heavy content
2. **Vertical integration** enables scale and supply chain control
3. **"Legacy brand" credibility** from pre-legalization presence
4. **Community positioning** ("for the culture") over celebrity worship
5. **Retail footprint** is its own marketing engine
6. **Embrace the controversy**—street cred can come from regulatory friction

### Compliance Reality:
- Meta platforms (Instagram, Facebook) remain hostile to cannabis content
- YouTube allows more educational/documentary content
- X (Twitter) offers most lenient paid ad policies for licensed operators
- Apparel/lifestyle content less likely to be flagged than direct product promotion
- Age-gating on social accounts is table-stakes compliance
