## 3. Massachusetts Cannabis Laws for Tourists (2026)

### Key Facts (sourced from Cannabis Control Commission + Mass.gov)

- **Legal age:** 21+ with valid government-issued ID (driver's license, passport, state ID)
- **Out-of-state visitors CAN buy:** Yes, recreational cannabis available to all adults 21+ regardless of residency
- **Possession limits (public):** Up to 1 ounce of flower OR up to 5 grams of concentrate
- **Possession limits (home):** Up to 10 ounces (must be locked if over 1 oz)
- **Purchase limits per transaction:** Up to 1 ounce (28g) of flower per day
- **Payment methods:** Cash or debit card only (no credit cards due to federal banking restrictions)
- **Public consumption:** ILLEGAL — private property only with property owner's permission
- **Public consumption fine:** Civil penalty up to $100
- **Open container in vehicle:** Illegal — up to $500 fine. Must be in closed container in trunk/locked glove box
- **Crossing state lines:** ILLEGAL regardless of destination state's laws
- **Airport/TSA:** Cannabis prohibited in airports and on planes (federal jurisdiction)
- **Hotel consumption:** Varies — must check hotel policy before consuming
- **Driving under influence:** Illegal — same DUI penalties as alcohol
- **Medical marijuana:** Out-of-state medical patients CANNOT purchase medical-grade products in MA

### New 2026 Laws: Social Consumption

- **Cannabis Control Commission approved social consumption regulations December 2025**
- **Effective date:** January 2, 2026
- **Three license types:**
  1. Supplemental licenses for existing dispensaries (lounges)
  2. Hospitality licenses for venues (cafes, yoga studios)
  3. Event organizer licenses for temporary events
- **Rules:** No alcohol/tobacco in consumption spaces; mandatory rideshare plans; employee training for impaired customers
- **Status:** Regulations finalized; individual businesses now applying for licenses. No lounges operational yet as of Feb 2026, but expected to open throughout 2026
- Massachusetts becomes the 12th state to permit some form of on-site cannabis consumption

## 4. East Boston Neighborhood Profile

### Demographics & Culture
- **Population:** ~47,000+
- **Hispanic/Latino:** 54%+ of residents (majority from Colombia, El Salvador, Guatemala)
- **History:** Created in the 1830s by connecting five islands; waves of immigrants — Canadians (1840s), Irish (1850s), Eastern European Jews and Italians (1890s), majority Italian by 1915, majority Latino since ~2000s
- **Known locally as:** "Eastie"
- **Key characteristic:** Immigrant neighborhood to its core; dense residential community separated from mainland Boston by harbor

### Key Landmarks & Attractions
- **Piers Park:** 11 acres, waterfront, sweeping views of Boston skyline, playground, fitness area, Piers Park Sailing Center. Prince William and Kate visited in 2023.
- **Constitution Beach (Shays Beach):** Views of harbor and planes at Logan, seasonal snack bar, playground, skating rink
- **Mary Ellen Welch Greenway:** 3.3-mile pathway from Jeffries Point to Constitution Beach
- **Bremen Street Park:** Linear park on abandoned railroad site, dog park, community events
- **LoPresti Park:** Basketball courts, hockey rink, turf soccer field, waterfront fishing, kayaking
- **Madonna Queen of the Universe Shrine:** 35-foot bronze/copper statue, built 1954 by Don Orione Fathers, spectacular views
- **The Tall Ship:** 245-foot floating oyster bar on Pier One, live entertainment, pop-ups
- **Downeast Cider:** Taproom in Eastie, one flight per guest

### Notable Restaurants
- **Santarpio's Pizza:** Cash-only, family-owned since 1903, visible from airport, Boston-style pizza
- **Angela's Cafe:** Colombian/Latin American cuisine
- **Rincón Limeño:** Authentic Peruvian restaurant, Maverick Square area
- **Taqueria Jalisco:** Mexican
- **KO Pies:** Australian meat pies
- **Maverick House Tavern:** 154 Maverick Street, trivia nights, open mic
- **Reelhouse:** Waterfront dining, raw bar, lobster
- **Eagle Hill Café:** Meridian Street coffee shop

### Transit & Access
- **MBTA Blue Line:** Maverick Station (1 stop from Airport Station), Orient Heights Station, Suffolk Downs Station, Wood Island Station
- **Maverick Station to downtown Boston:** ~5 minutes (one underwater stop to Aquarium)
- **Airport Station to Maverick Station:** 1 stop, ~3 minutes
- **Logan to CH by car:** ~5 minutes via Neptune Road
- **East Boston Ferry:** Service to Seaport, ~$5
- **Massport Shuttle:** Free shuttle connects all Logan terminals to Airport Blue Line station

### Cultural Notes
- **Veronica Robles Cultural Center:** In former East Boston Library on Meridian Street, Latino cultural hub
- **Eastie Week:** Annual community celebration with restaurants, events
- **Eastie Farmers Market:** Seasonal community market
- **Gentrification concerns:** Rising rents pushing longtime Latino families to Revere, Lynn; culturally shifting neighborhood
