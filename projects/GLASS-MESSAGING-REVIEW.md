# GLASS Website Messaging Review & Rewrite

**Date:** February 12, 2026  
**Reviewer:** Product Strategy & UX Copy  
**Audience lens:** "Maria" — 45, owns 2 dispensaries in Western MA, non-technical, runs from phone, cares about margins and staff, bounces if it feels like SaaS marketing.

---

## Executive Summary

The GLASS site has strong bones — real data, real product, clear visual hierarchy. But it's talking to a product manager at a SaaS company, not to Maria. Almost every page leads with *what the product does* instead of *what Maria gets*. The language is technical ("real-time pricing intelligence," "AI-powered detection," "multi-tenant ready"), the structure rewards scrollers instead of scanners, and the ecosystem feels like four separate pitch decks stapled together.

The fix is a tone shift, not a rebuild. Maria needs to feel like she's talking to someone who runs a store, not someone who builds software.

## Part 2: Ecosystem Narrative

### The Problem With the Current Narrative

Right now the site presents four separate products that happen to share data. The user experience is:

1. Land on GLASS → learn about price tracking
2. Scroll to ecosystem cards → see four products with "Live" or "Coming Soon" badges
3. Click into one → get a completely separate pitch page
4. Come back → repeat

There's no story connecting them. No reason Maria would want *all four* instead of just the one she clicked on.

### The Through-Line

Every dispensary owner has the same four problems, in this order:

1. **"Am I priced right?"** → GLASS answers this
2. **"Are customers finding what they want on my website?"** → Virtual Budtender answers this
3. **"Are my budtenders actually trying?"** → Cue answers this
4. **"Should I offer delivery?"** → Delivery answers this

The through-line is: **Run a smarter dispensary, one layer at a time.**

Each product is a layer. You start wherever your biggest pain is. The data connects them so each one gets smarter the more you use.

### How to Present It

Don't present four products. Present **four capabilities of one platform.**

The site should feel like: "GLASS is the intelligence layer. These are the things it powers."

**Analogy Maria would get:** GLASS is like having a really smart operations manager who watches the market, helps your website sell, keeps your staff motivated, and expands your delivery reach — except it costs $49/month instead of $65K/year.

### Narrative Structure for the Homepage

1. **Hero:** One problem, one promise. "Know what your competitors charge. Every day."
2. **How it works:** Three steps. Sign up → See your market → Act on it.
3. **The four capabilities:** GLASS, Budtender, Cue, Delivery — each with a one-sentence hook.
4. **Real data proof:** Numbers that demonstrate this is real, not vaporware.
5. **CTA:** "See My Market Free"

### Avoiding "Four Products Bolted Together"

- Use consistent design language across all four product pages (same layout template, same CTA style)
- On each product page, show how it connects to the others — but as a small aside, not a pitch
- Use shared navigation that signals "you're in one ecosystem" (e.g., sidebar or breadcrumb)
- Let Maria discover the other products naturally through the dashboard, not through product pages

## Part 4: Cross-Product Messaging Inside the Dashboard

### Principles
- Introduce other products only when they're contextually relevant
- No banner ads. No pop-ups. No "have you tried...?" interruptions
- Use the data as the bridge: "You're seeing X in your pricing data. Here's something that could help."

### GLASS Dashboard → Virtual Budtender

**When to introduce:** When Maria is looking at her product catalog or pricing data.

**Copy:**
> 💡 **Your website could recommend products like these automatically.** Virtual Budtender puts a product recommender on your site — customers answer 4 questions, get matched to products from your actual menu. Free to set up. [Learn more →](/budtender)

**Alternative — after she sees her top sellers:**
> **These are your most popular products.** Want customers on your website to find them faster? [See how Virtual Budtender works →](/budtender)

### GLASS Dashboard → Cue

**When to introduce:** When Maria is looking at product performance or competitive gaps.

**Copy:**
> 📊 **These products are priced above market.** Want your budtenders to push them? Cue lets you set up incentive programs — "sell 10 of these this week, earn a $20 bonus." Brands often fund the rewards. [See how Cue works →](/cue)

**Alternative — if she has underperforming products:**
> **This product hasn't moved in 2 weeks.** One dispensary used a Cue incentive and sold 40 units in 5 days. [Try a budtender incentive →](/cue)

### GLASS Dashboard → Delivery

**When to introduce:** When Maria sees competitors who offer delivery and she doesn't.

**Copy:**
> 🚚 **3 of your nearby competitors offer delivery. You don't.** We're building a way for you to offer delivery without a license. [Get on the waitlist →](/delivery)

### Tone Rules for Cross-Product Hints
- One hint per dashboard page, max. Never two.
- Always tied to her data. Never generic.
- Always dismissable (X button, "don't show again").
- Never the first thing she sees. Data first, hint second.
