# REVIEW PLAN C — Feedback: The Maria Test

**Reviewer:** Senior Product Strategist (Small Business Owner Lens)  
**Date:** February 12, 2026  
**Subject:** Kimi's GLASS Ecosystem Domination Strategy — Through the Eyes of a Real Dispensary Owner  
**Verdict:** The strategy is architecturally brilliant and commercially dead on arrival. It's a plan written for investors, not for the person who has to open the register at 9am.

---

## 1. Overall Assessment

### What's Strong

1. **The data is real and rare.** 92K+ products, 492 operators, 31K CCC records spanning 7 years — this is a genuine competitive moat. Nobody else has cross-referenced real-time shelf pricing with historical CCC data and pending license applications. That's genuinely valuable.

2. **The flywheel logic is sound.** GLASS → Budtender → Cue → Delivery → more data → better GLASS. As a business model, this makes sense. The compounding loops are real.

3. **The revenue math is directionally correct.** Blended $279/operator/month is plausible if the product delivers clear value. The margin structure (94% gross margin) is healthy.

4. **The "Bloomberg of Cannabis" positioning is smart.** Owning the reference benchmark for pricing is a powerful moat — if you can actually earn that trust.

5. **The state expansion playbook is practical.** Gating criteria before replication is the right instinct. Too many startups expand before proving the model.

### What's Missing

1. **Maria.** She's not in this document. Not once. There's no user persona. No usage scenario. No morning routine. No "what does the operator actually do with this?" The plan describes what the system does, not what the human experiences.

2. **Plain language.** The entire document is written in a language that alienates the buyer. "Demand Intent Graph," "Elasticity Panel," "Narrative Intelligence Dataset," "License Pipeline Heatmap + Threat Conversion Model." These phrases would make Maria close the browser tab.

3. **The 60-second value moment.** Nothing in this plan describes what happens when Maria first opens the app. What does she SEE? What does she FEEL? What makes her come back tomorrow? The plan jumps straight to infrastructure without ever describing the human experience.

4. **The phone.** The word "mobile" appears zero times. Maria runs her business from her phone. She checks things between customers. She doesn't sit at a desk with a big monitor studying D3 visualizations.

5. **Simplicity.** 19 D3 visualizations. 5 data moat layers. 6 monetization layers. 4 product modules. 12-month quarterly roadmap. This is complexity porn. Maria needs three things: "Am I okay?", "What should I worry about?", and "What should I do?"

6. **Trust mechanics.** The plan talks about becoming infrastructure. Infrastructure requires trust. Trust requires proof. The plan never addresses: "Why should Maria believe these numbers?" Especially given the data quality issues (fake sparklines, unit mismatches, garbage brand data) flagged in Reviews A and B.

7. **Competitor reality.** Maria already uses Dutchie. She already uses Blaze or Treez. She's already overwhelmed by tools. The plan never addresses: "How does GLASS fit into Maria's existing stack?" or "What tool does GLASS replace?"

### What Needs to Change

The entire plan needs to be inverted. Instead of:

> "Here's what the platform can do" → "Here's what data we need" → "Here's the infrastructure"

It should be:

> "Here's what Maria needs to know right now" → "Here's how we show her" → "Here's the data behind it"

Technology-out thinking vs. human-in thinking. The plan is the former. The product needs to be the latter.

## 5. The Notification Strategy

### The Rules
1. **Maximum 3 notifications per week.** More than that and Maria mutes us.
2. **Every notification must be actionable in 10 seconds.** If she can't do something about it immediately, don't send it.
3. **Use dollar amounts, not percentages.** "$400" means something. "8.3% above market" doesn't.
4. **Use plain English.** No jargon. No marketing speak.
5. **Channel: SMS for urgent, email for weekly digest.** Maria reads texts. She skims email.

### SMS Notifications (Max 2/week)

**Price Alert:**
> "Heads up — Happy Valley just dropped flower prices 15%. 3 stores near you already matched. Want to see your options? [link]"

**Opportunity Alert:**
> "GLASS found $320/month in pricing opportunities for your store. 2 products are priced too low. Tap to see which ones. [link]"

**New Competition Alert:**
> "A new dispensary just applied for a license in Worcester. That would make 15 shops within 5 miles of you. [link]"

**Never Send:**
> ❌ "Your Demand Intent Graph shows a 23% shift in consumer preference vectors toward the indica-dominant segment."
> ❌ "The MA Retail Price Index moved -0.4% WoW with a confidence interval of ±0.2%."
> ❌ "Your competitive positioning percentile shifted from 73 to 68 across monitored categories."

### Weekly Email (Monday at 7am — "Your Monday Market Update")

**Subject line:** "Worcester Market Update: 3 things to know this week"

**Body:**
```
Hey Maria,

Here's what happened near you this week:

1. PRICES MOVED 📉
   Average flower price in Worcester dropped $2 this week.
   You're still in a good spot — 3rd cheapest out of 14 stores.

2. YOUR OPPORTUNITY 💰
   Your Sleep Gummies ($31) are priced $6 below market average ($37).
   Bumping to $35 would still keep you competitive and add ~$120/month.

3. HEADS UP 👀
   A new dispensary application was filed near downtown Worcester.
   Probably 12-18 months away, but worth watching.

That's it. Have a good week.

— GLASS

[Open my dashboard →]
```

**Format rules:**
- Max 3 items per email
- Each item: one emoji, one headline, 2-3 sentences
- Total read time: under 2 minutes
- Always end with a warm close, not a sales pitch
- Include "Open my dashboard" link, not "Upgrade to Pro"

### Monthly Digest (First Monday of the month)

**Subject:** "January recap: How your store did"

**Body:**

## 6. The Word-of-Mouth Moment

### What Makes Maria Tell Another Owner

Maria won't tell her friend about GLASS because of a beautiful dashboard. She won't tell her friend because of network effects or data moats. She'll tell her friend about GLASS because of one of these three moments:

#### Moment 1: "I found money I was leaving on the table"
Maria adjusts her pre-roll prices based on a GLASS recommendation. A week later, her margins are up. She texts her friend: "Have you heard of GLASS? They showed me I was undercharging on pre-rolls by $3. Already making an extra $400/month."

**How to engineer this:** Make the Store Report Card and opportunity alerts so specific and so easy to act on that Maria actually changes a price. Then follow up: "Hey Maria — since you adjusted pre-roll prices 5 days ago, it looks like the market is still above your new price. Nice move."

#### Moment 2: "I saw my competitor's price change before they announced it"
Maria gets a text: "Happy Valley just dropped their flower to $35." Maria checks — it's real. She adjusts before customers notice. She feels like she has a superpower. She tells her friend: "I knew about Happy Valley's price drop before it was even on their website. This app is like having a spy."

**How to engineer this:** Price change alerts within 24 hours of detection. SMS, not email. Specific: which competitor, which product, what price, what your price is.

#### Moment 3: "I avoided a mistake I didn't see coming"
Maria sees the notification: "3 new dispensary applications in Worcester." She starts planning — maybe increases her marketing, maybe diversifies her product mix. Six months later, two of those shops open. She was ready. She tells her friend: "GLASS warned me about those new shops 6 months before they opened. I was the only one who wasn't surprised."

**How to engineer this:** The pending license alert is the easiest "wow" moment to deliver because we ALREADY HAVE the data (234 pending applications in the database). Surface it, personalize it by city, and Maria will feel like she's getting insider information.

### What Doesn't Create Word-of-Mouth
- ❌ Beautiful visualizations (operators don't share dashboards)
- ❌ "Market Intelligence Platform" branding (operators don't evangelize platforms)
- ❌ Elasticity panels and demand intent graphs (operators don't understand them)
- ❌ Free tools that are cool but not actionable (operators forget about them)

### The Referral Mechanic
Don't build a referral program. Build a product so specific that Maria can screenshot it and text it to a friend. The Store Report Card — the green/yellow/red pricing comparison — is inherently sharable because it's personal, visual, and surprising.

If we had to build a mechanic:
> "Know an operator who should see this? Send them their own store report. It's free."
>
> [Send to a friend →]

This lets Maria give her friend the same "holy shit" moment she had — personalized to THEIR store, generated from data we already have, no signup required to view.

### Phone Design Rules

1. **No charts on mobile.** Replace every chart with a number + a color + one sentence. "Your flower is $38 avg. Market is $36. You're slightly above. 🟡"
2. **Maximum 3 taps to any insight.** Home → Category → Product detail. That's it.
3. **Thumb-friendly.** All tap targets 48px minimum. Bottom navigation. One-handed operation.
4. **Pull to refresh.** Maria wants to feel like she's checking something live.
5. **No scrolling past 3 screens.** If she has to scroll more than 3 screen-heights, the page is too long.
6. **One CTA per screen.** Not three. Not "Apply / Edit / Dismiss." One thing to do.
7. **Loading should feel instant.** Skeleton screens, cached data from last visit while fresh data loads.
8. **Dark mode default after onboarding.** Easier on the eyes at 6:30am.

## 9. The Anti-Dashboard — What Replaces Complex Visualizations

### The Problem
Kimi's plan features 19 D3 visualizations:
1. Price Position Matrix
2. Volatility Ribbon
3. Brand Treemap
4. Category Sunburst
5. Economic Indicators
6. Strain Stream
7. Price Change Cascade
8. Price Ticker
9. Proximity Radar
10. Market Share Flow (Sankey)
11. Price War Heatmap
12. Market Terrain Map
13. Delivery Voronoi
14. Threat Network
15. CCC Timeline
16. License Pipeline
17. Weekly Sales Chart
18. Cross-Reference Panel
19. Sparklines

Maria would not use a single one. Not because they're bad — because they require interpretation. Maria doesn't interpret charts. She needs answers.

### The Anti-Dashboard: Replace Every Chart with a Sentence

| Visualization | Replace With |
|---|---|
| Price Position Matrix | "Your flower is $38. Market average is $36. You're 5% above." |
| Volatility Ribbon | "Prices in your area moved a lot this week" / "Prices have been stable" |
| Brand Treemap | "Top brands near you: 1. Fernway 2. Good Chemistry 3. Bountiful Farms" |
| Category Sunburst | "Most stores near you focus on flower (45%), edibles (22%), and vapes (18%)" |
| Economic Indicators | "The overall market is getting cheaper. Down 3% this month." |
| Strain Stream | (Remove. Not actionable.) |
| Price Change Cascade | "Happy Valley dropped flower 8% this week. NETA raised vapes 5%." |
| Price Ticker | (Remove. Real-time tickers are for day traders, not dispensary owners.) |
| Proximity Radar | "14 stores within 5 miles. Closest: Happy Valley (0.8 mi)" |
| Market Share Flow | (Remove. "Market share" from SKU count is misleading and confusing.) |
| Price War Heatmap | "No active price wars in your area" / "Worcester flower is seeing aggressive price cuts" |
| Market Terrain Map | "14 stores near you. 4 new applications pending." [Simple map with dots] |
| Delivery Voronoi | (Remove. Save for when delivery actually launches.) |
| Threat Network | "3 things to watch near you" [simple list] |
| CCC Timeline | "Timeline: Jan 15 — new application filed. Dec 8 — license approved." [simple feed] |
| License Pipeline | "4 applications pending in Worcester. Probably 12-18 months away." |
| Weekly Sales Chart | "Massachusetts dispensaries sold $XX million last week" (one number, not a chart) |
| Cross-Reference Panel | "You charge $42 for flower. The state average is $36. That's 17% more." |
| Sparklines | (Remove ALL fake sparklines. Replace with "↑ up this week" / "↓ down" / "→ stable") |

### The Rule: If it needs a legend, kill it.

If Maria needs to read a legend, hover over tooltips, or mentally parse axes to understand what a visualization is saying, it's wrong. Replace it with a sentence.

The only acceptable visualizations for Maria:
1. **Simple bar comparison** — "Your price" vs "Market average" (two bars, that's it)
2. **Red/yellow/green indicators** — status at a glance
3. **A map with dots** — "Here are stores near you" (no Voronoi, no heat, just dots)
4. **A simple list** — "Recent changes near you" (newest first)

Everything else? Sentences. Numbers. Colors. Plain English.

### The Desktop Exception

For power users who DO want charts (maybe 10% of users — multi-location operators, data-minded owners), offer an "Advanced View" toggle. But default to simple. Always simple.

## Final Verdict

Kimi's plan is the right machine. But it's missing the human interface.

The architecture is sound. The flywheel is real. The data moat is valuable. The revenue model works. But none of that matters if Maria opens the app and sees "Demand Intent Graph" and "Elasticity Panel" and closes it forever.

**The single most important change:** Invert the entire product from technology-out to human-in. Start with what Maria sees on her phone at 7:30am. Make that so clear, so simple, and so valuable that she comes back at 7:30am tomorrow. Then build everything else underneath.

The strategy is the engine. Maria is the driver. Right now we've built a Formula 1 engine and handed the keys to someone who drives a minivan. Not because Maria is unsophisticated — because she has more important things to do than learn your dashboard.

Make it simple. Make it personal. Make it about her money.

Everything else follows.
