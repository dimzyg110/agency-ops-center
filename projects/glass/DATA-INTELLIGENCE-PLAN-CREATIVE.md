### 1. 🔥 "The Fraud Machine" — Quality Assurance Intelligence

**What it is:** Cross-reference lab test results across dispensaries to expose quality fraud at scale.

**The dirty secret of cannabis testing:** Labs compete for business. Cultivators shop for favorable results. The same batch shows up at different stores with different THC percentages. Some "28% THC" flower is actually 19%. Everyone in the industry knows this. Nobody can *prove* it — until now.

**How GLASS proves it:**
- Match products across stores using the product matching engine (same brand + strain + batch number)
- Compare lab results for identical batches sold at different retailers
- Build a **Lab Integrity Score** — which labs consistently produce inflated results?
- Track cultivators who switch labs frequently (a red flag)
- Identify "THC inflation corridors" — geographic clusters where reported potency is statistically impossible

### 6. The Universal Product Database — "Clearbit for Cannabis"

**What it is:** Build the canonical database of every cannabis product in America. Standardized names, categories, attributes, lab results, pricing history, supply chain provenance.

**Why this matters:** Cannabis product data is a disaster. The same product is listed 50 different ways across 50 stores. "Blue Dream 3.5g" vs "Blue Dream Flower 1/8oz" vs "BLUE DREAM (flower) — eighth." There's no UPC system. No SKU standard. Nothing.

**GLASS's product matching engine is the solution.** And whoever builds the canonical product database owns the data layer for the entire industry.

**The business model:**
- **Free API tier:** Let any developer look up products (like Clearbit's free company lookup) → builds adoption and dependency
- **Paid API:** Enriched data (lab results, pricing, supply chain) → $500-$5,000/mo based on volume
- **Enterprise:** Custom integrations, POS system plugins, compliance tools → $2K-$25K/mo
- **The real play:** Become the *standard* that POS systems, compliance platforms, and state regulators all reference. When GLASS assigns a product ID, that becomes the ID. Like how DUNS numbers work for businesses.

**Network effect:** Every operator that connects → more products matched → better matching → more operators connect. Classic data network effect.

**Revenue potential:** $3-10M/yr from API access alone. But the strategic value is incalculable — you become infrastructure.

**How this gets built:**
- Product matching engine (fuzzy matching, NLP, category taxonomy) is the technical core
- Start with MA's 100K products, expand to every state as you enter them
- Partner with POS systems (Dutchie, Treez, Flowhub) to ingest product data automatically
- Assign GLASS Product IDs (GPIDs) — the cannabis industry's UPC

**Priority: HIGHEST** — This is the other $100M play. It compounds with every other initiative. Build it first.

## PART IV: THE TECHNICAL CORE — WHAT MAKES ALL OF THIS POSSIBLE

Everything above is vapor without three technical systems. Here's what they enable and why they're worth building perfectly:

### System 1: Product Matching Engine (The Foundation)

**What it does:** Takes the chaos of 100K+ product listings across 490+ dispensaries and resolves them into canonical product entities.

**Why it's the most important system:** Every single revenue stream above depends on being able to say "this product at Store A is the same product at Store B." Without matching, you have 490 separate spreadsheets. With matching, you have a market.

**Technical approach:**
- **Stage 1 — Text normalization:** Strip packaging language, standardize units (3.5g = ⅛ oz = eighth), normalize brand names
- **Stage 2 — Fuzzy matching:** TF-IDF + cosine similarity on product names, with category and brand as hard filters
- **Stage 3 — Lab result correlation:** Same batch number + similar cannabinoid profile = high-confidence match
- **Stage 4 — Supply chain confirmation:** Same cultivator + same batch + same period = definitive match
- **Stage 5 — Human-in-the-loop:** Flag low-confidence matches for review, feed corrections back into the model

**The "Clearbit" play:** Assign every matched product a **GLASS Product ID (GPID)**. Publish a free lookup API. When POS systems, compliance platforms, and state regulators start referencing GPIDs, you've won. You're infrastructure.

**Accuracy target:** 95%+ precision (false matches are worse than missed matches in this domain — a wrong match could mean wrong lab results attributed to a product).

**What this unlocks:**
- Cross-store price comparison (Pricing Intelligence)
- Lab result anomaly detection (Fraud Machine)
- Supply chain tracing (who grows what, who sells it)
- Consumer search (find this product near me)
- Market sizing (how big is the "live rosin" category, really?)

---

### System 2: Supply Chain Intelligence Graph (The Moat)

**What it does:** Maps relationships between entities (cultivators, processors, distributors, retailers, brands) and tracks product flow between them.

**Data model:**
```
[Cultivator] --grows--> [Batch]
[Batch] --tested_by--> [Lab] --produces--> [Lab Result]
[Batch] --processed_by--> [Processor] --creates--> [Product]
[Product] --distributed_to--> [Retailer]
[Retailer] --sells_to--> [Consumer (anonymous)]
[Entity] --owned_by--> [Owner/Company]
[Owner/Company] --also_owns--> [Other Entity]
```

**Implementation:** Neo4j or similar graph database. The relationships ARE the product. SQL can't express "show me every entity within 2 ownership hops of this PE firm."

**What this unlocks:**
- **Ownership Graph** (#2): Traverse ownership relationships to expose hidden connections
- **Supply chain risk:** If a major cultivator fails, which retailers are exposed?
- **Diversion detection:** Products appearing at retail without a matching upstream source
- **Market concentration analysis:** HHI scores by market, category, supply chain position
- **M&A target identification:** "This cultivator supplies 40% of the top-20 stores and nobody knows who they are yet"

---

### System 3: Pricing Intelligence Engine (The Cash Cow)

**What it does:** Tracks every price change across every product at every store, over time, and generates actionable intelligence from the patterns.

**Core capabilities:**
- **Price history for every product at every store** (time series database — InfluxDB or TimescaleDB)
- **Market price calculation:** Median price per product across all stores, by region
- **Price anomaly detection:** Stores pricing 30%+ above or below market → why?
- **Elasticity modeling:** When Store A drops price on Product X, what happens to Store B's sales of the same product?
- **Promotional intelligence:** Detect when stores run sales, track which promotions actually drive volume
- **MAP monitoring:** Are retailers respecting minimum advertised prices? (Brands will pay for this.)

**What this unlocks:**
- **For retailers:** "You're charging $45 for something the store 2 miles away sells for $35. Here's what happens to your sales when you do that."
- **For brands:** "12 of your retail partners are selling below your MAP. Here are the receipts."
- **For regulators:** "These 6 stores changed prices on the same 15 products on the same day. That's a price-fixing pattern."
- **For consumers:** "This product is $10 cheaper on Tuesdays at Store C" (dynamic pricing intelligence)
