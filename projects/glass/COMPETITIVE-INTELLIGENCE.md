## 1. Executive Summary

The cannabis data intelligence space is more crowded than it appears at first glance — but almost nobody is doing what GLASS plans to do. Here's the landscape in 30 seconds:

**Tier 1 — Enterprise Data Giants ($15K-$75K+/yr):** Headset, BDSA, New Frontier Data. They sell market-level intelligence to brands, MSOs, and investors. They have POS-level transaction data from thousands of retailers. They are NOT competing with GLASS — their customers are enterprise brands, not independent dispensary operators. Too expensive and too macro for a 5-store MA owner.

**Tier 2 — Menu Intelligence Scrapers ($500-$2K/mo):** Hoodie Analytics, Lit Alerts, Pistil Data. These are the **closest competitors to GLASS's Price Wire**. They scrape dispensary menus, track pricing/availability, and sell to brands AND retailers. Lit Alerts launched in MA in late 2023 and charges $500/mo per market. Hoodie tracks 10,000+ dispensaries nationally and just partnered with High Times for consumer-facing product discovery.

**Tier 3 — License/CRM Data ($45-$500+/mo):** CannaSpyglass, Cannabiz Media. License databases and B2B intelligence. Not competing with GLASS.

**Tier 4 — Marketing/Loyalty ($300-$900/mo):** Springbig, AlpineIQ, Happy Cabbage, Stemless, Leafbuyer. SMS/email marketing and loyalty for dispensaries. Adjacent to GLASS but not competing on intelligence.

**The Critical Gap Nobody Fills:**
- **Consumer-facing cross-platform price comparison** — Nobody offers consumers a way to compare the same product across dispensaries, adjusted by distance/price/availability. Weedmaps and Leafly show individual store menus but don't match products across stores. Hoodie's High Times partnership is the closest attempt.
- **Universal Product Matching (GPID)** — LucidGreen's LucidID ($14.5M raised) is the only company attempting product identity for cannabis, but it's supply-chain focused (QR codes on physical packages), not menu-level matching.
- **Operator-first free intelligence** — Every data platform charges operators. GLASS's free core tier is a fundamentally different go-to-market.

**GLASS's Unique Position:** We are the only platform that combines (a) scraping ALL menu platforms (not just one), (b) free operator intelligence, (c) consumer-facing price comparison, and (d) integrated fulfillment spokes (delivery, brand incentives, toll processing). Nobody else has this architecture.

**What:** Menu intelligence platform that crawls public dispensary menus and aggregates competitive pricing, inventory alerts, and sales velocity estimates.

**Founded:** Late 2023, launched in MA first  
**Founder:** Nathan Girard (Owner of Bloom Brothers dispensary in Pittsfield, MA & Menands, NY)  
**HQ:** Massachusetts  
**Markets:** 15 states (started in MA, expanded west)  
**Funding:** Bootstrapped (no disclosed funding)  

**Pricing:** $500/month per market. No per-seat limits. 30-day free trial. No long-term contracts. API access included.

**What They Do:**
- Crawl publicly available dispensary menus during business hours
- Aggregate data into one dashboard
- Automated inventory & out-of-stock alerts
- Real-time estimated sales & sell-through velocity
- Competitive pricing & price-trend tracking
- New market and product launch analysis
- Daily data updates (hourly menu crawling)

**Target Customer:** Primarily wholesale brands/manufacturers and secondarily retailers. Their testimonials are almost entirely from Directors of Sales, VPs, CMOs at wholesale cannabis companies.

**Strengths:**
- Founded by an actual dispensary operator (credibility)
- MA-native — knows this market intimately
- Simple pricing ($500/mo flat per market)
- Fast-growing (15 markets in ~2 years)
- Strong testimonials from MA brands (The Heirloom Collective, etc.)
- API access included

**Weaknesses:**
- Wholesale-focused — not building for independent dispensary operators
- $500/mo is expensive for a single-store operator
- No free tier
- No consumer-facing product
- No delivery/fulfillment integration
- No AI pricing recommendations (shows data, doesn't recommend action)
- No CCC/regulatory data integration
- Unknown technical depth of product matching

**GLASS vs Lit Alerts:**
| Dimension | Lit Alerts | GLASS |
|-----------|-----------|-------|
| Price | $500/mo per market | Free core |
| Target | Brands/wholesale | Independent operators |
| Data | Menu scraping | Menu scraping + CCC + Google + lab data |
| Action | Alerts only | Alerts + AI pricing recommendations |
| Fulfillment | None | Delivered + AC Toll + Cue |
| Consumer facing | No | Yes (price comparison) |
| Product matching | Unknown | GPID cross-platform matching |

**Strategy: Compete aggressively.** Lit Alerts targets brands; GLASS targets operators. But if Lit Alerts pivots to retailers, they're our #1 threat in MA. Our free tier undercuts them completely. Our integrated spokes (delivery, Cue) create a moat they can't easily replicate.

- **HIGH TIMES PARTNERSHIP** — Powering consumer-facing cannabis shopping and discovery on HighTimes.com (compare pricing, place pickup/delivery orders)

**Target Customer:** Brands, distributors, MSOs, retailers, technology providers (POS companies embed Hoodie data)

**Strengths:**
- Massive national scale (10,000+ dispensaries)
- Deep data moat (3 billion daily observations, foot traffic, POS integrations)
- $5M+ funded with institutional backing
- High Times partnership = consumer distribution channel
- Machine learning for product conformity across volatile cannabis naming
- Been operating since 2018 — years of historical data
- Serves the entire supply chain (brands, distributors, retailers, tech partners)

**Weaknesses:**
- Enterprise pricing excludes independent operators
- Not MA-focused (national = generic)
- Not operator-first (brands are primary customer)

**GLASS vs Hoodie:**
Hoodie is the player we need to watch most long-term. They have the data, the scale, and now a consumer-facing play via High Times. But:
1. They're national and generic; we're MA-specific and deep
2. They charge enterprise prices; we're free for operators
3. Their consumer play is brand-driven ("Where to Buy"); ours is consumer-driven ("Find the best price")
4. They don't have delivery or fulfillment spokes
5. They don't integrate CCC regulatory data

**Strategy: Partner before they compete.** Hoodie is too big to fight head-on. But they don't have MA depth. Explore partnership where Hoodie provides national market intelligence and GLASS provides MA-specific operator tools. If partnership fails, our free tier + local depth + integrated spokes create a defensible niche they won't prioritize.

## 6. GLASS Unfair Advantages

### 6.1 Cross-Platform Menu Scraping (THE #1 ADVANTAGE)
Every other data company is either:
- **Platform-specific** (Dutchie only shows Dutchie menus, Weedmaps only shows WM menus)
- **POS-dependent** (Headset/BDSA need retailers to share their POS data)
- **Single-source scrapers** (Lit Alerts and Hoodie scrape public menus but don't cross-reference)

**GLASS scrapes Dutchie + Weedmaps + iHeartJane + Leafly + custom sites, then matches products across platforms.** This means:
- We see the SAME product priced differently at the SAME store on different platforms
- We see products that appear on one platform but not another
- We can build the GPID (Global Product ID) — the universal cannabis product catalog
- We can tell operators: "Your competitor is $3 cheaper on this exact product" with high confidence

**Nobody else is doing this.** Hoodie and Lit Alerts scrape menus but don't appear to cross-reference across platforms. Headset has transaction data but only from opted-in retailers. BDSA has menu analytics but enterprise-priced.

### 6.2 Free Core = Network Effect Weapon
Every competitor charges operators. GLASS is free.
- Lit Alerts: $500/mo
- Hoodie: $1K-5K+/mo (est.)
- Headset: $250+/mo
- Happy Cabbage: $900/mo

In a market where wholesale prices have collapsed 72% and margins are razor-thin, asking operators to pay $500+/mo for data is a hard sell. GLASS's free tier removes all friction. This accelerates adoption, which accelerates data density, which accelerates recommendations quality.

### 6.3 CCC Regulatory Data Integration
No competitor integrates Massachusetts Cannabis Control Commission data:
- 772 licensed operators with contact info
- Monthly average pricing data
- Lab testing results (298K rows — THC/THCA accuracy)
- Pending applications (Threat Radar)
- Enforcement actions
- Weekly sales reports

This gives GLASS the **THC Accuracy Score** (cross-reference lab results vs menu claims) and **Threat Radar** (new licenses, violations) — features nobody else can replicate without doing this same data integration.

### 6.4 Integrated Fulfillment Spokes
GLASS is not just intelligence — it's intelligence + action:
- **Cue** — Brand-funded incentives (operators pay $0, brands pay to influence)
- **Delivered** — Delivery-as-a-service (toggle delivery on/off, exclusive license MD1303)
- **AC Toll Processing** — White-label manufacturing
- **Roviq** — Agency services

No data platform has fulfillment. No fulfillment platform has data. GLASS bridges both.

### 6.5 Exclusive Delivery License
Delivered's license MD1303 (exclusive social equity until 2029) means no competitor can replicate the "Toggle Delivery" spoke without obtaining their own delivery license in an extremely limited licensing environment (only ~27 delivery operators in all of MA).

### 6.6 Consumer-First Strategy
Every other cannabis data company is B2B. GLASS's consumer-facing price comparison (find the best price for any product near you) creates:
- A consumer demand channel that operators WANT to be listed on (free marketing for them)
- A data flywheel (consumer searches reveal demand signals)
- A brand-new category — "Kayak for cannabis"
- SEO opportunity (consumers searching "best price [strain] near me")

Hoodie's High Times partnership is the closest to this, but it's brand-driven (brands pay for placement) not consumer-driven (best price wins).

## 8. Gaps Nobody Is Filling

### 8.1 Consumer-First Cross-Platform Price Comparison
**Gap:** No consumer-facing tool lets you search "Blue Dream near Boston" and see every dispensary's price for that product, ranked by price and distance, with availability status.

Weedmaps and Leafly show individual store menus but don't match products across stores. Hoodie/High Times does product discovery but it's brand-driven placement, not consumer-driven price comparison.

**GLASS fills this:** GPID-based product matching + consumer-facing search. "Kayak for cannabis."

### 8.2 Universal Cannabis Product ID (GPID)
**Gap:** LucidGreen's LucidID is physical (QR codes on packages). Metrc Retail ID is compliance. Nobody has built a **digital** universal product identifier that maps "Sour Diesel 3.5g by XYZ Brand" across Dutchie, Weedmaps, iHeartJane, Leafly, and custom sites to the same canonical product.

**GLASS fills this:** Our fuzzy matching + normalization pipeline creates the GPID catalog. This becomes the backbone for price comparison, product tracking, and cross-platform analytics.

### 8.3 Operator Intelligence at $0
**Gap:** Every intelligence tool charges operators. The operators who need data the most (independent single-store owners fighting MSO competition) can least afford it.

**GLASS fills this:** Free core forever. Revenue from brand-funded spokes (Cue), delivery (Delivered), and toll processing (AC).

### 8.4 Regulatory Intelligence as a Feature
**Gap:** No data platform integrates state regulatory data (CCC in MA) as a real-time feature. Pending licenses, enforcement actions, violation history — all public but nobody aggregates it for operators.

**GLASS fills this:** Threat Radar combines CCC filings, meeting minutes, license applications, and enforcement actions into actionable alerts. "New competitor opening 2 miles from you in 90 days."

### 8.5 Intelligence → Action → Fulfillment Pipeline
**Gap:** Data companies show you the problem. You have to figure out what to do. Nobody goes from "competitor dropped prices on edibles" → "here's your recommended price change" → "apply it" → "activate brand incentive to offset margin" → "turn on delivery for that zip code."

**GLASS fills this:** Price Wire → Margin Engine → Cue → Delivered. Intelligence → Recommendation → Incentive → Fulfillment. One platform.

### 8.6 THC Accuracy Score
**Gap:** Menu-listed THC percentages often differ from lab-tested results. CCC has 298K lab testing rows. Nobody cross-references these against live menu claims.

**GLASS fills this:** THC Accuracy Score catches label fraud, builds consumer trust, and creates a moat nobody can easily replicate (requires both live menu data AND lab testing data).

### 8.7 MA-Specific Depth
**Gap:** National platforms (Hoodie, Headset, BDSA) cover MA but don't go deep. They don't know which municipal meetings matter, which license applications are pending in your town, or what the CCC's price data says about your specific category in your specific region.

**GLASS fills this:** Hyper-local MA intelligence that national players will never prioritize.
