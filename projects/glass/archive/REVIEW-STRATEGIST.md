## 3. PRICING STRATEGY

### Is $199/mo Right?

**$199/mo is in the right ballpark but should be your entry point, not your only tier.**

Context from the competitive landscape:
- Happy Cabbage: $900/mo (AI revenue optimization)
- Headset: Enterprise pricing (typically $500-2000+/mo)
- BDSA: $3K+/year for dashboards, much more for daily insights
- POS systems (Dutchie, Flowhub, Treez): $300-800/mo
- BLAZE: ~$500/mo

MA dispensaries with 100+ products are doing $1-5M/year in revenue. A tool that saves them even 2% on pricing optimization = $20-100K/year in recovered margin. $199/mo ($2,388/year) is a rounding error.

### Recommended Tier Structure

| Tier | Price | What They Get | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Market View** (Free) | $0 | Public market page, basic store report card (no alerts, monthly refresh) | Lead gen. Get them in the door. Build trust. |
| **Operator** | $149/mo | Daily report card, weekly email digest, competitor price alerts (email only), CSV exports, 5-mile competitor view | Independent single-location operators who are price-sensitive but need intelligence |
| **Operator Pro** | $299/mo | Everything in Operator + SMS alerts, real-time competitor monitoring, 15-mile radius, lab test verification, license threat alerts, priority support | Multi-location or high-revenue operators who need the full picture |
| **Enterprise** | Custom ($500+/mo) | API access, custom integrations, white-label reports, dedicated account management | MSOs, consulting firms, investors |

### Why This Structure

1. **Free tier is your growth engine.** 772 operators get a free report card. 20% convert to paid. That's 150 paid customers at $149-299/mo = $22K-45K MRR.

2. **$149 entry reduces objection.** $199 feels like a deliberate decision. $149 feels like "less than my monthly POS fee." Psychologically easier to say yes.

3. **$299 Pro tier is where the margin is.** The delta between $149 and $299 is SMS + lab verification + expanded radius. Cost to serve is ~$5/mo more. The lab test verification alone justifies the jump — if you can show an operator that 3 products on their shelf have inflated THC numbers, that's worth $299/mo in liability protection alone.

4. **Enterprise for the consulting play.** Cannabis consultants, investors doing due diligence, MSOs evaluating MA markets. They'll pay $500+/mo without blinking if the data is unique.

### First Revenue Optimization

**Don't launch with 4 tiers.** Launch with:
- Free (report card, no alerts)
- $199/mo (everything, 14-day trial)

Simplify until you have 20+ paying customers. Then segment based on what you learn about usage patterns. Over-segmenting at 0 customers is premature optimization.

## 5. THE LAB TEST ANGLE: How Aggressively Should You Lean In?

### Why This Matters

THC inflation is a documented, industry-wide scandal:
- MJBizDaily found **routine inflation by 25%+ across 4 states** (Nov 2023)
- Colorado researchers found **nearly half of tested flower exaggerated THC levels** (Oct 2025)
- NY just cracked down on a lab with stiff penalties (Sep 2025)
- FiveThirtyEight documented labs caught inflating, passing moldy cannabis, and fabricating results

You have **147K CCC-verified lab results** that can be cross-referenced against what dispensaries claim on their menus. If a product is listed as 32% THC on Dutchie but CCC lab results show the batch tested at 24%, that's an 8-point discrepancy. At scale, this is either:
- **Consumer protection:** Operators selling mislabeled product (legal liability)
- **Competitive intelligence:** Operators using honest testing vs. operators using "friendly" labs
- **Brand quality scoring:** Which brands consistently test within 2% of labeled potency?

### My Recommendation: Lean In Hard, But Carefully

**Do:**
- Build a "Label Accuracy Score" for every product where you can match menu claims to CCC lab data
- Surface this to operators as a quality differentiator: "Your menu claims are 96% accurate. Your competitor across the street? 78%."
- Frame it as protection, not accusation: "GLASS helps you verify what you're selling matches what was tested"
- Use it as a PR/content angle: publish aggregate findings (anonymized) showing the MA market's accuracy rate. This gets press coverage. MJBizDaily would cover this.
- Make it a Pro tier feature: Label accuracy scores justify the $299/mo Pro plan

**Don't:**
- Don't name-and-shame specific operators publicly. You'll get sued and blacklisted.
- Don't frame it as "catching cheaters." Frame it as "protecting your reputation."
- Don't overstate accuracy of your matching — batch-to-menu matching has inherent timing uncertainty. Be transparent about confidence levels.
- Don't lead with this in outreach. Lead with pricing intelligence (it's less threatening). Introduce lab verification as a value-add after trust is established.

### The Big Play

If you can demonstrate that GLASS identifies mislabeled products before regulators do, you become a compliance tool, not just an intelligence tool. Compliance tools have stickier retention and higher willingness-to-pay. The CCC itself might eventually be interested in this data.

**Timeline:** Don't build this in February. Build it in April, after you have 20+ paying customers and stable data. It's a killer expansion feature, not a launch feature.

- "Your edible pricing is 18% below market. That's either smart traffic-driving or margin you could recover."]

### Why This Works

1. **Subject line is about THEM, not you.** Curiosity + specificity.
2. **Body leads with their data.** Proves you actually know their business.
3. **"No sales pitch" is true.** GLASS core is free. You're giving, not asking.
4. **The P.S. is the conversion weapon.** A detail so specific they think "wait, how does this person know this?" That P.S. gets more clicks than the main CTA.
5. **Personalized URL removes all friction.** They click, they see their data, zero signup required.

### What NOT to Do

- Don't send from a no-reply address. Send from randy@roviqai.xyz or similar.
- Don't include more than one CTA. The only action is "see your report card."
- Don't mention pricing, tiers, or paid features in the first email. Ever.

## 9. THE 511K-ROW ADVANTAGE: Communicating Data Depth Without Sounding Like a Database Vendor

### The Problem

"We have 511,000 rows of data" means nothing to an operator. It sounds like a tech flex. Operators care about *their store*, not your database.

### The Translation

| What You Have | What the Operator Hears |
|---|---|
| 511K rows of data | "We track every dispensary in Massachusetts" |
| 100K products | "We know what every store near you sells and what they charge" |
| 147K lab tests | "We can verify if products on your shelf match their lab results" |
| 67K sales records | "We have 7 years of actual sales data showing exactly how this market evolved" |
| 71 months of price data | "We can show you where prices are headed — because we've watched them since day one" |
| 234 pending applications | "We know who's trying to open next to you before they break ground" |
| 46K plant records | "We can see supply changes before they hit the market" |

### The Messaging Framework

**Never say:** "Our database has 511K rows" or "We have the most data"

**Always say:** "We track every dispensary, every product, every price change in Massachusetts — and cross-reference it against 7 years of regulatory data"

**The power phrase:** "We already know your store." This implies depth without quantifying rows. When an operator types their name and instantly sees their full menu analyzed with competitor context, the data speaks for itself.

### Content Marketing Angle

Publish specific insights from the data. Not "we analyzed 511K rows." Instead:

- "Massachusetts flower prices dropped 73% since 2018. Here's the month-by-month breakdown."
- "The 5 most oversaturated zip codes for MA dispensaries (and the 5 most underserved)"
- "Which MA cannabis brands appear in the most dispensaries? We tracked 100K products to find out."
- "THC percentages on MA menus vs. CCC lab results: how accurate are your labels?"

Each of these is a LinkedIn post, a newsletter issue, and an SEO page. The data speaks; GLASS gets credit.
