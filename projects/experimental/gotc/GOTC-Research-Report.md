# Game of Thrones: Conquest (GOTC) — Comprehensive Research Report

*Compiled February 7, 2026*

---

## 1. Game Overview

Game of Thrones: Conquest is a real-time strategy mobile MMO developed by Warner Bros. Games, launched in October 2017. Players build strongholds, train armies, and compete with alliances (called "Allegiances") to control Seats of Power across a persistent map of Westeros. The game has surpassed **$1 billion in cumulative player spending** as of early 2025, making it one of the most commercially successful IP-based mobile strategy games ever made.

Current monthly performance sits around **$1.5M combined** (iOS ~$900K, Google Play ~$600K), with an estimated **93K active players**. The game has refreshed engagement through **House of the Dragon** content integration, adding heroes like Rhaenyra Targaryen and Alicent Hightower, dragons Syrax and Caraxes, and 140+ dragon customization options.

## 2. Core Gameplay Mechanics

### 2.1 Base Building

The **Keep** is the foundational building — no other structure can exceed its level. Everything in the game scales with Keep progression.

**Key building categories:**

- **Resource Production:** Farms (Food), Sawmills (Wood), Stone Quarries (unlocked at Keep 10), Iron Mines (unlocked at Keep 15)
- **Military:** Barracks (Infantry), Range (Ranged), Stable (Cavalry), Workshop (Siege Engines), War Camp (training capacity)
- **Support:** Medic Tent (wounded troop healing), Storehouse (resource protection from raids), Maester's Tower (research), Dragon Pit
- **Strategic:** Watchtower (march speed/defense), Great Hall (rally capacity), Shrine (daily resource prayers)

### 2.2 Combat System — Rock-Paper-Scissors

Three primary troop types form a counter triangle:

- **Infantry** beats Cavalry (high defense, slow movement)
- **Cavalry** beats Ranged (high speed, mobile flanking)
- **Ranged** beats Infantry (distance damage advantage)

Additional types include **Siege Troops** (wall-breaking specialists) and **Trap Troops** (base defense). The recommended approach is sending at least 6 different troop types per attack to avoid hard counters, while specializing your primary and secondary types for stat optimization.

Combat is further modified by dragon abilities (Fire Breath for offense, Repel Strike for defense), gear stat bonuses, research tree investments, and consumable boosts.

### 2.3 Seats of Power & Territory Control

Westeros is divided into 8 regions (the Seven Kingdoms plus Crownlands), each containing a Capital seat (4–4.5 stars) and supporting seats (1–3.5 stars). Under **Region Control** rules, an allegiance must hold enough lower seats within a region before they can march on the Capital — making even 1-star seats strategically important.

Controlling the **Iron Throne** grants 30% Infantry Attack plus Construction Speed, March Speed, and Dead-to-Wounded conversion bonuses to the holding allegiance.

### 2.4 Resources & Economy

Four primary resources (Food, Wood, Stone, Iron) drive all building, training, and research. Secondary resources include Gold (premium currency), building materials (Brick, Soldier Pine, Keystone, Valyrian Stone), and various speedup items. Resources beyond your Storehouse capacity are vulnerable to raiding, creating constant tension between growth and exposure.

## 3. Advanced Techniques & Strategy

### 3.1 Rally Mechanics

Rallies are the core offensive coordination tool. A designated leader (typically the highest-Keep player with the largest Great Hall capacity) initiates a rally against a target. Alliance members join by committing troops. Key techniques:

- **Gear swapping:** Leaders use march gear while the rally fills, then switch to attack gear before the rally launches
- **Composition balancing:** Rally participants coordinate troop types to avoid collective counter-weaknesses
- **Title assignment:** Attacker Titles provide strategic bonuses to the rally force

### 3.2 Reinforcement Timing

When defending a Seat under attack, allegiance members send reinforcement waves. Effective defense requires keeping teammates geographically close for fast troop replenishment, investing in Watchtower march speed, and coordinating multiple reinforcement waves to prevent sequential elimination.

### 3.3 Shield Management

Peace shields (3 minutes post-Seat conquest, 72-hour cooldown) and purchased shields are critical survival tools. F2P players are heavily dependent on shield timing. Advanced play involves tracking enemy shield expirations and coordinating attacks during vulnerability windows.

### 3.4 Trapping (Sandbagging)

A deceptive strategy where players keep their visible "power" artificially low while secretly training strong armies. This involves keeping troops in healing tents, maintaining lower building levels than capacity allows, and hiding research advancement. The goal is to bait stronger players into attacking what appears to be a weak target, only to encounter unexpected resistance.

### 3.5 March Speed Manipulation

March speed determines how much reaction time opponents get. The optimization hierarchy is: Watchtower (best for SoP marches) > Shrine (general speed) > War Supplies research (troop-specific). Faster marches catch opponents AFK or unprepared.

### 3.6 Research Priorities

The Maester's Tower offers five research disciplines (Military, Advanced Military, City Defense, Logistics, Economy). Optimal priority sequence:

1. **Bureaucracy** — building speed compounds over time
2. **War Supplies** — march speed advantage in PvP
3. **Economy tree** — essential for F2P/low-spender resource generation
4. **March slot unlocks** — expand simultaneous action capacity

### 3.7 Gear & Dragon Optimization

Gear ranges from Poor to Legendary quality. Top players specialize by stacking stats on one primary troop type rather than spreading bonuses thin. Dragon talent trees progress through Hatchling, Whelp, Adolescent, and Adult phases, with the Adult tree offering the highest PvP value (especially "vs Player Troop Type" stats and Fire Breath wall-melting).

## 4. Progression & Monetization

### 4.1 Progression Curve

- **Early Game (Keeps 1–10):** Focus on Food/Wood production, basic troops, foundational research. Cheap and fast progression.
- **Mid Game (Keeps 11–20):** Stone and Iron unlock, expanded troop capacity, advanced research. Resource acquisition becomes difficult without spending around Keep 16.
- **Late Game (Keeps 21+):** High-tier troops only, premium research, gear forging optimization, dragon training, competitive SoP control. Recent level expansions support Keep ranges 34–40.

### 4.2 P2W vs F2P Reality

The game is technically F2P but competitive viability increasingly requires spending. F2P players need strong allegiance support to survive and cannot keep pace with monthly gear releases. Recent litigation has argued that advancement through in-game labor alone becomes "all but impossible." That said, dedicated F2P accounts exist primarily within allegiances that specifically protect and coordinate around non-spenders.

**Key P2W levers:** Keep acceleration, speedups, gear crafting materials, VIP status (permanent multipliers), and direct market purchases.

## 5. Social Dynamics & Discord Integration

### 5.1 Alliance (Allegiance) Structure

Allegiances operate with a 5-tier hierarchy: R5 (Leader), R4 (Officers), R3 (Key Members), R2/R1 (Standard Members). Maximum 100 members per allegiance. R4s handle membership, rank management, research projects, shop restocking, map markers, and military coordination. The feudal "Bannermen" system allows lords to recruit up to 5 direct reports, creating loyalty chains.

### 5.2 Why Discord Is Essential

Most serious allegiances use Discord over in-game chat for its superior features: persistent conversation history, channel organization, bot integration, voice chat for live coordination, and better file/media sharing. Typical alliance Discord structures include announcement channels, rally coordination channels, KvK planning channels, strategy guides, and social/off-topic spaces.

**Key Discord bots used in the GOTC ecosystem:**

- **HodorBot** — troop count tracking, level monitoring, event participation stats
- **Groupflows** — cross-timezone event scheduling with RSVP functionality
- **GroupUp** — guild event scheduling and rally coordination

**Notable community Discord servers:**

- **Official GOTC Discord** — 80,810+ members, central hub for announcements
- **The Citadel** — independent library/tutorial community with guides and giveaways
- **GOTCTips.com Discord** — community tips, previews, and update discussions

### 5.3 Kingdom Politics & Diplomacy

**NAP (Non-Aggression Pact)** agreements between allegiances are the backbone of kingdom diplomacy. They allow allied allegiances to focus on mutual threats rather than internal conflict. Broken NAPs trigger kingdom-wide wars. Cross-kingdom negotiations, coalition formation, and intelligence networks create a rich political layer that mirrors the Game of Thrones IP.

### 5.4 Migration System

Monthly **"Month of Migration"** events feature two mechanics:

- **Kingdom Merge** — two kingdoms combine; players are shielded during transition, keeps randomly placed, Seats of Power reset
- **Stake Your Claim (SYC)** — a kingdom splits, and players choose which new kingdom to join, enabling strategic political repositioning

These migrations constantly reshape the social landscape and drive engagement as new power dynamics emerge.

### 5.5 Events That Drive Social Engagement

**Kingdom vs Kingdom (KvK)** connects two similarly-aged kingdoms for cross-server warfare. It includes a 24-hour Invasion Phase and a 32-hour Open War Phase, with exclusive rewards (KvK Pawn Shards). A cross-kingdom chat tab opens, enabling trash talk and diplomacy with opponents.

**Alliance Conquest** pits two allegiances against each other over 9 contested locations (Casterly Rock, strongholds, outposts, temples). Officers register during a 1-day window and select match times. Victory is determined by battle points from occupying locations.

**The Daily Raven** — an in-game newspaper reporting kingdom events, tracking alliance victories, and celebrating player achievements, creating narrative within the game world.

### 5.6 Community Culture & Resources

The community emphasizes both cooperation and betrayal, mirroring the GoT universe. Spying on rival allegiances, dramatic alliance switches during battles, and political intrigue are all part of the culture. Key community resources include the Fandom Wiki (523 pages, 170+ articles), David's Guides to Conquest, GOTCTips.com, and a Player Council that gives constructive feedback to developers (credited with features like Preset Managers and the SYC system).

**Common slang:** SOP (Seats of Power), KvK (Kingdom vs Kingdom), Zeroed (attacked until teleported away), Bubble (protection shield), RSS (resources), Rally, Bannermen, Reinforcements.

## 7. Game Theory in GOTC and the Genre

### 7.1 Prisoner's Dilemma in Alliance Politics

Every alliance member faces a choice between cooperation (contributing resources/troops to alliance goals) and defection (hoarding for personal advancement). In a one-shot game, defection dominates. But because GOTC is a **repeated game** — players interact with the same alliance members for months or years — cooperation emerges naturally through reputation effects and tit-for-tat dynamics. Alliances enforce cooperation through kick policies, contribution tracking, and rank-based reward distribution.

### 7.2 Free Rider Problem

Alliance resource pools can be built collectively, but rational individuals are incentivized to minimize contributions while maximizing benefits. GOTC addresses this through donation rewards (personal benefit for contributing), public contribution leaderboards (social pressure), rank-based perk distribution, and expulsion mechanisms for chronic non-contributors. The failure of LOTR: Rise to War illustrates what happens when pay-to-win mechanics make lower-spending members' contributions meaningless — the free rider problem becomes unsustainable and alliances collapse.

### 7.3 Nash Equilibrium in Combat

Troop composition choices create a game-theoretic optimization problem. The rock-paper-scissors mechanic means no single troop type is universally optimal — the best choice depends on what opponents field. This creates a meta-game where dominant strategies shift as players discover and counter them. Developers must balance troop types to maintain equilibrium and prevent degenerate strategies.

### 7.4 Hawk-Dove Dynamics in Territory Control

Players choose between aggressive expansion (Hawk) and defensive development (Dove). If most players are Doves, Hawks prosper through uncontested aggression. If most are Hawks, Doves prosper while aggressors exhaust each other's resources. The equilibrium naturally produces a mix of strategies — which is exactly the political diversity seen in mature GOTC kingdoms.

### 7.5 Signaling & Reputation

Visible metrics (war contributions, donation leaderboards, damage dealt, event participation) serve as costly signals of reliability. High contributors get preferential loot access, officer promotions, and premium alliance invitations. This creates self-reinforcing reputation dynamics where cooperation becomes rational for long-horizon players. Conversely, players approaching departure or migration dates tend to defect (the "end-of-game effect").

### 7.6 Collective Action in Siege Warfare

Major objectives like Capital seats require sustained coordination from many players. Individual incentives to free-ride (benefit from others' losses without contributing) threaten collective success. GOTC solves this through real-time event mechanics, leader coordination tools, and shared reward structures that align individual and collective incentives.

## 8. Key Takeaways

1. **GOTC is fundamentally a social game.** While base-building and combat are the surface mechanics, the real game is alliance coordination, kingdom politics, and cross-server diplomacy. Discord integration has become essential infrastructure.

2. **The IP is both asset and liability.** Game of Thrones provides instant brand recognition and thematic depth (politics, betrayal, power dynamics), but creates dependency on HBO's franchise health and limits the game's ability to evolve independently.

3. **Monetization balance is a competitive advantage.** GOTC's moderate approach (relative to Lords Mobile or Rise of Kingdoms) may be why it reached $1B lifetime while LOTR: Rise to War — with comparable IP strength — collapsed after aggressive P2W changes.

4. **Game theory is not just academic.** Prisoner's dilemma, free rider problems, and signaling dynamics are playing out in real-time across every kingdom. Understanding these frameworks gives players a genuine strategic advantage.

5. **The genre is mature but resilient.** Top games (Clash of Clans, Rise of Kingdoms, Lords Mobile) are 5–12 years old and still generating hundreds of millions annually. The social bonds created by alliance systems produce retention that pure gameplay cannot match.

6. **Migration systems are a social innovation.** Kingdom Merges and Stake Your Claim events constantly refresh the political landscape, preventing stagnation and giving players agency over their social environment — a feature most competitors lack.
