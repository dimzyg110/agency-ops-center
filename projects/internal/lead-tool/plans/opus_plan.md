## Part 4: Key Bets & Assumptions

### What We're Betting On

1. **Distressed operators will pay for help**
   - Assumption: Operators with 40-100 distress scores have enough revenue to afford $3K
   - Evidence: Good reviews = foot traffic = revenue. Distress ≠ broke, it = stressed
   - Risk: Some are too far gone. Qualification calls essential.

2. **The toll processing gap is real and monetizable**
   - Assumption: 35+ cultivators genuinely need this and would pay for introductions
   - Evidence: They have licenses, they're growing, but no mfg = leaving margin
   - Risk: Some may have already found partners or given up

3. **Data subscriptions will sell to vendors/brands**
   - Assumption: Cannabis vendors will pay $299-499/mo for pre-qualified dispensary lists
   - Evidence: Industry databases (Headset, Leafly Pro) charge $500-2,000/mo
   - Risk: Market may be too small or buyers may DIY

4. **The Sprint can close fast with distressed operators**
   - Assumption: 14-day close cycle is achievable with right targets
   - Evidence: Distress = urgency. Good reviews = not defensive about product.
   - Risk: Cannabis operators are notoriously flaky/slow

### What Could Go Wrong

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|-------------|--------|------------|
| Target operators can't actually pay | Medium | High | Qualify hard on call, ask about runway |
| Competition sees same opportunity | Low | Medium | Move fast, build relationships, own the niche |
| Wholesale prices keep falling, everyone fails | Medium | High | Focus on operational efficiency, not just marketing |
| Data goes stale, signals become less accurate | Low | Medium | Weekly updates already running |
| Randy burns out doing everything solo | High | High | Automate early, raise prices, say no to bad fits |

### Hedges

1. **If Sprint sales are slow:** Pivot to lower-ticket Intel Packs ($500) as entry point, upsell later
2. **If toll processing doesn't take off:** Kill it fast, focus on Sprint + data
3. **If no one wants subscriptions:** Sell data as one-time reports ($500-2,500) instead
