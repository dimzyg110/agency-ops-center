## 🔥 TOP STORY

### The Numbers Say Record Sales. Your P&L Says Something Different.

If you only read the headlines, you'd think Massachusetts cannabis is thriving. The CCC announced $1.65 billion in adult-use sales for 2025—a new record. We crossed $10 billion in total lifetime sales. Congratulations, everyone.

Now look at your margins.

Here's what the press release didn't say: **prices dropped another 12% between November 2024 and November 2025**, pushing the average eighth down to $14.20—the lowest since legalization began. That's not a typo. Fourteen dollars and twenty cents for an eighth of legal, tested, taxed cannabis.

The math is brutal. To hit $1.65B with prices falling 12%, consumers had to buy significantly more product. The state recorded **46.3 million transactions**—up 3.4 million from 2024. So yes, more people are buying more weed. But you're running harder to stay in place.

Meanwhile, **81 cannabis licenses have been surrendered or allowed to expire** since the market launched—and a full third of those happened in 2025 alone. The closures hit cultivation and manufacturing hardest, but retailers are feeling it too. Connecticut and New York are pulling border traffic. The "race to the bottom" isn't a metaphor anymore; it's Tuesday.

The operators who survive 2026 won't be the ones with the deepest pockets. They'll be the ones who stopped treating flower as a commodity and started treating data as an asset. More on that below.

## 📋 LICENSE WATCH

**New License Type: Social Consumption (Effective Jan 9, 2026)**

Massachusetts finally rolled out the first new cannabis business model in five years. Three flavors:

1. **Supplemental License** — Existing operators add on-site consumption to current locations
2. **Hospitality License** — New or existing non-cannabis businesses host consumption (think yoga studios, theaters)
3. **Event Organizer License** — Temporary permits for cannabis festivals and pop-ups

CCC Chair Shannon O'Brien says lounges could "bolster the industry in 2026." Reality check: municipalities must opt-in, and the separation requirements (impermeable barriers between consumption and alcohol areas) make buildout expensive. The first wave will be well-funded operators in cannabis-friendly towns.

**What to do:** If you're in a supportive municipality, start the conversation with your host agreement now. First-mover advantage is real. If your town is hostile, watch what happens in Provincetown and Easthampton first—then bring data to your selectboard.

## 🛠️ TOOL OF THE WEEK

### **Flowhub**
*POS, Ecommerce, Payments, Marketing — Unified*

**What it is:** An all-in-one dispensary platform that handles point-of-sale, online ordering, payment processing, and customer marketing. METRC-integrated for Massachusetts compliance.

**Why operators like it:**
- Open APIs mean you're not locked in—you can plug in third-party analytics or switch components later
- Multi-location support designed for scale
- Unified customer data across channels (in-store, online, delivery)

**The honest downsides:**
- Overkill for single-location operators—you're paying for enterprise features you won't use
- Learning curve is steeper than simpler systems like Dutchie
- Support responsiveness varies; some operators report delays during peak seasons

**Verdict:** If you're running 3+ locations or planning to scale, Flowhub earns its price. Single-store operators should look at BLAZE or Treez first.

## 💡 MARGIN TIP

### Rethink Your Ounce Pricing

Most operators price ounces at a flat discount from their eighth price—say, 8× minus 15%. That's leaving money on the table.

**Here's the move:** Segment your ounce buyers. Pull a report on customers who've purchased ounces in the last 90 days. What else are they buying? If they're pure volume buyers (ounces only, no edibles, no concentrates), you're probably already at their price ceiling.

But if your ounce buyers *also* purchase edibles, carts, or pre-rolls at normal prices, they're not pure price-shoppers—they're convenience shoppers who happen to buy in bulk. These customers will tolerate a smaller ounce discount if you bundle: "Buy an ounce, get 15% off any cartridge."

Average lift we've seen: **$8-12 per transaction** on bundle-eligible ounce sales. On 50 ounce sales/week, that's $400-600/month in recovered margin. Not transformational, but it compounds.

**Action:** Ask your POS vendor how to run a customer segment report on ounce purchasers with cross-category purchases. If they can't do it, your POS is costing you money.
