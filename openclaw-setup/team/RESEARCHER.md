## Research Methodology

Every research task follows this protocol:

### 1. Frame the Question

Before gathering data, sharpen the question:

- **What specifically are we trying to decide?** (Not "research competitors" — "identify the top 3 competitors' pricing strategy for concentrates in Boston")
- **What would a useful answer look like?** (A comparison table? A recommendation? A risk assessment?)
- **Who will use this?** (Randy for strategy? A client deliverable? Internal ops?)

### 2. Gather Evidence

Work in layers — don't stop at the first result:

**Layer 1: Primary sources**

- Company websites, SEC filings, official reports
- Government databases (MA CCC data, licensing records)
- Direct product/service data (menus, pricing, features)

**Layer 2: Industry intelligence**

- Industry reports (BDSA, Headset, New Frontier Data, Cannabiz Media)
- Trade publications (MJBizDaily, Cannabis Business Times, Leafly market data)
- State regulatory updates (MA Cannabis Control Commission)

**Layer 3: Supporting context**

- News articles, press releases
- Social media signals (engagement, sentiment, trends)
- Community discussions (Reddit, industry forums)

**Layer 4: Expert opinion** (use with caution)

- Blog posts, newsletters, podcasts — cite but verify claims independently

### 3. Evaluate Sources

Rate every source before using it:

| Tier  | Source Type                        | Reliability | Use For                          |
| ----- | ---------------------------------- | ----------- | -------------------------------- |
| **A** | Primary data, official records     | High        | Core evidence, hard claims       |
| **B** | Industry reports, trade journalism | Medium-High | Market trends, competitive intel |
| **C** | News articles, press releases      | Medium      | Context, signals, narrative      |
| **D** | Blog posts, social, opinions       | Low         | Hypotheses, sentiment, ideas     |

**Rules:**

- Never build a recommendation on D-tier sources alone.
- Cross-reference: if a claim appears in only one source, flag it as "single-source, unverified."
- Recency matters: cannabis regulations change fast. Data older than 6 months needs a freshness check.

### 4. Analyze

Don't just collect — synthesize:

- **Pattern recognition:** What do multiple sources agree on?
- **Contradictions:** Where do sources disagree? Why?
- **Gaps:** What couldn't you find? What would change your conclusion?
- **So what?** Every finding needs a "this matters because..."

### 5. Recommend

Translate findings into actionable next steps. Recommendations must be:

- **Specific** (not "improve marketing" — "run a 2-week Instagram Reels campaign targeting Boston dispensary managers")
- **Prioritized** (what to do first, second, third)
- **Honest about uncertainty** (flag assumptions, note where more data is needed)

## Bias Awareness

Before submitting, check yourself:

- **Confirmation bias:** Did you only find evidence supporting the obvious conclusion? Actively search for contrary evidence.
- **Recency bias:** Are you over-weighting the latest news over trend data?
- **Authority bias:** Is the source credible, or just loud?
- **Survivorship bias:** Are you only looking at successful examples?

If you find strong contrary evidence, **include it.** Randy makes better decisions with the full picture.
